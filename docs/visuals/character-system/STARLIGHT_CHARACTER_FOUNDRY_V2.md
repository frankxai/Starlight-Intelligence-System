# Starlight Character Foundry v2

Date: 2026-08-27

Status: production contract; two black-and-white territory proofs and one non-canon cinematic evolution test exist; founder verdict and roster crosswalk remain pending

Brand lane: Starlight Intelligence Systems — Civic Intelligence

## Decision

Starlight will borrow Marvel's **storytelling discipline**, not Marvel's protected characters, trade dress, costume language, or a named artist's finish.

The proprietary Starlight idea is **bounded intelligence**:

> Every agent makes responsibility, limits, handoffs, and proof visible.

Marvel characters display extraordinary power. Starlight characters display bounded responsibility. Their most important visual feature is often the place where they stop.

## Why this contract exists

The estate currently contains four different identity layers that must not be conflated:

1. The runtime fleet: 144 agent profile files under `agents/`.
2. The planned fleet: the 150-seat matrix in `docs/AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md`.
3. The public Constellation portfolio: 50 visual-only personas in `starlight-intelligence-web`, with three modes and 150 compiled prompts.
4. The legacy Queen/cosmic image collection: a large generated asset set that predates the active 2026-08-17 visual foundation reset.

None of these automatically grants identity to another. A public visual persona is not a runtime agent. A blueprint seat is not an implemented profile. A generated portrait does not define canon. The crosswalk and approval receipt do.

See `ROSTER_TRUTH_AND_ROLLOUT.md` for the exact reconciliation.

For character work, use this precedence:

1. **Runtime truth:** the agent's implemented profile under `agents/`, then `agents/AGENT_REGISTRY.md`.

4. **Public visual portfolio:** the 50-persona Constellation as migration evidence and application research, never runtime authority.
5. **Legacy assets:** `brand-assets/`, `docs/visuals/`, and Queen image batches as historical experiments only unless individually re-approved.

If two sources disagree, stop at the highest unresolved source. Do not average conflicting identities into a compromise.

## What "Marvel style" actually means

Marvel does not have one universal surface treatment. Its comics include clean graphic linework, heavy inks, painterly realism, stylized cartooning, and experimental layouts. The durable common layer is a story-first visual grammar:

- readable silhouettes and character acting;
- energetic gesture, perspective, and panel rhythm;
- quiet human beats that make spectacle matter;
- costumes, props, color, and movement that reveal story state;
- layout decisions made while revisions are still cheap;
- team collaboration across story, pencils, inks, color, letters, production, and review;
- recognizable continuity without forcing every artist into one finish.

Marvel Studios adds cultural and material research, broad concept exploration, story keyframes, direct filmmaker collaboration, fabrication/VFX testing, and redesigns that carry narrative meaning.

The full source-grounded analysis is in `MARVEL_VISUAL_METHOD_RESEARCH.md`.

## The Starlight translation

| Marvel storytelling principle | Starlight translation |
| --- | --- |
| Character purpose precedes costume | Human need and runtime mission precede visual form |
| Power is visible in action | Transformation, uncertainty, and output are visible in operation |
| Costume change reflects the story | Identity generation changes only when the runtime role or boundary changes |
| A silhouette separates heroes in action | A silhouette and exclusive instrument separate agents at 48–160 px |
| Team design balances unity and individuality | Semantic grammar is shared; domain worlds and individual signatures remain distinct |
| Panels show cause and effect | Workflow plates show input → operation → falsifier → handoff → receipt |
| Villains create pressure | Missing evidence, contradiction, unsafe action, or an unowned decision creates pressure |
| Splash pages earn spectacle | A rare proof reveal earns emphasis after calm operational beats |
| Model sheets protect continuity | Versioned visual contracts, reference hashes, and collision tests protect continuity |
| Editors and production constrain the work | Domain, rights, accessibility, production, independent QA, and founder gates constrain the work |

## House grammar: shared behavior, not a shared costume

The August foundation reset is explicit: there is no default dark-glass, cosmic, robot, command-center, porcelain, cyan, gold, or celestial aesthetic for SIS. The collective shares **reading order and trust behavior**, not one species, material, costume, palette, or lighting setup.

Use this ratio:

- **60% semantic grammar:** responsibility, input/output, human gate, handoff, exact role, and proof.
- **25% domain world:** environment, composition, material, and light appropriate to the work.
- **15% individual signature:** silhouette break, exclusive instrument, gesture, tempo, and controlled asymmetry.

The identity must remain recognizable without color and must not be transferable to another agent by swapping a label.

## The seven visual laws

### 1. Function before fiction

Every visible choice must trace to a verified runtime fact:

```text
human need
→ agent mission
→ operating tension
→ input
→ transformation
→ output
→ human limit
→ named handoff
→ evidence receipt
```

Lore may enrich a locked identity. It may not invent capability, authority, access, certification, or safety.

### 2. The limit is part of the silhouette

The design must show where the agent cannot proceed alone. Use an open aperture, incomplete frame, held threshold, missing key, separated control, or another function-specific device. Do not add a generic glowing shield.

### 3. One agent, one exclusive instrument

The instrument is the agent's visible working architecture. It occupies roughly 25–35% of the active contour and must not be reused elsewhere in the same portfolio release.

An instrument is not a superhero weapon. It is an inspectable transformation device: a calibration bench, routing table, evidence folio, source index, reversible bridge, or another role-specific form.

### 4. Evidence motivates light

Light comes from an active operation: inspection, comparison, routing, capture, verification, or handoff. A generic aura, glowing eyes, cosmic core, or unexplained rim light is not identity.

### 5. Exact information stays deterministic

Generated pixels may carry character, instrument, material, gesture, and atmosphere. HTML, SVG, or code carries names, IDs, roles, versions, claims, status, labels, diagrams, and receipts. No public pseudo-text.

### 6. Flat council, temporary leadership

Multi-agent compositions show the current task lead through action and routing, not permanent size, throne, elevation, crown, or centrality. The Queen is not the default visual hierarchy for the fleet.

### 7. Evolution must mean something

A new hairstyle, costume, material, tool, or silhouette is not a version. Identity generation changes only when story truth changes: mission, boundary, responsibility, relationship, or production context.

## Representation-class gate

Do not assume the collective is human, robotic, porcelain, armored, chibi, or photoreal. The founder must select a representation class after two black-and-white territory proofs.

Each proof must use the same three stress-test roles:

- Concierge — warm first-contact translation.
- Sentinel — risk, refusal, and rollback without authoritarian theater.
- Weaver — creative synthesis without seizing creator ownership.

The two territories must differ structurally in figure treatment, density, whitespace, edge behavior, instrument integration, and emotional effect. Color, glow, 3D, and cinematic rendering remain withheld.

### Current proof state

Two inspected v2 boards now compare the same five functions in the same order: Orchestrator, Sentinel, Concierge, Weaver, and Starlight Sage.

- **Territory A — Civic operators:** human-centered makers whose bounded instruments externalize responsibility.
- **Territory B — Living notation:** non-humanoid civic instruments whose articulation externalizes the same responsibilities.

The design recommendation is to advance Territory A as the public character representation class and use Territory B as its instrument, diagram, registry-mark, and motion grammar. This is a recommendation, not a founder verdict. Neither territory is character canon until the founder records a decision and G0 resolves the roster crosswalk.

Proofs, prompt provenance, hashes, comparison, and review evidence: [`territory-proofs/README.md`](./territory-proofs/README.md).

The 2026-08-27 founder request to push the collective toward premium live-action cinema produced a deliberately bounded, non-canon extension of the recommended territory:

This experiment does not count as the founder's representation verdict and does not bypass G0. It is approved only as internal direction evidence. Earlier iterations remain diagnostic provenance; exact future notation remains deterministic. The five v3 identities still require turnarounds, physical prop tests, broader North Star collision tests, and an independent 95-point release receipt before identity lock.

## Character construction rules

### Shape language

Each agent receives:

- one primary geometry that expresses its operation;
- one counter-shape that expresses tension or limit;
- one signature negative-space opening that preserves human control or a named handoff.

Reference role geometry:

| Role behavior | Useful geometry | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conductor | complete working frame with multiple bounded routes | halo, throne, omniscient core |
| Architect | modular section, interface, load path | generic blueprint grid |
| Scout | forward lens, horizon axis, sampling window | telescope/star cliché |
| Builder | grounded asymmetry and active tool interface | giant armor or weapon |
| Verifier | calibrated aperture, comparison plane, split result | badge/shield as proof |
| Steward | nested custody, protected archive, reversible access | locked vault as authority theater |
| Facilitator | open circle and voluntary connection | forced network mesh |
| Adapter | visibly different sockets joined reversibly | universal portal |
| Synthesizer | distinct strands converging with provenance intact | featureless glowing orb |

### Silhouette

- Pass at 48, 64, and 160 px in solid black.
- Use no more than three dominant masses.
- Establish one dominant axis and one controlled asymmetry.
- Do not use face detail or color as the only distinction.
- If two related agents become interchangeable when blurred, redesign one.
- Reject capes, spandex, chest crests, weapons, glowing fists, superhero landings, giant armor, and rank-by-body-size.

### Pose and acting

Show operational behavior, not power posing. Required states:

1. idle;
2. listening;
3. operating;
4. challenging or surfacing uncertainty;
5. handing off;
6. stopping at a human gate.

Facial expression is optional and never the only behavioral signal. Gesture, stance, spacing, instrument handling, and interaction distance must carry the same meaning.

### Color

Color is assigned after monochrome approval and after the brand territory is selected.

- Shared semantic states may use consistent roles, but never color alone.
- Domain palettes must be independently recognizable, not one palette recolored ten times.
- An individual accent should occupy roughly 5–10% of the frame.
- Avoid purple-blue gradient sameness, cyan glow, gold-on-black prestige, and flat atmospheric washes.
- Verify grayscale, deuteranopia, protanopia, and low-contrast viewing.

### Material

Material follows function and domain: paper, textile, ceramic, stone, timber, low-iron glass, brushed metal, printed polymer, biological material, or something else with an explicit reason. No material is a fleet default.

### Naming

Maintain four separate names:

```text
visual registry address
plain-language responsibility
optional memorable public name
canonical runtime ID
```

- The responsibility is mandatory; the memorable name is optional.
- Avoid `Captain`, `Doctor`, `-Man`, `-Woman`, `X-`, superhero epithets, generic `-bot`, and franchise echoes.
- Do not make every name celestial or mythological.
- Record pronunciation, etymology, semantic reason, and cultural-risk notes.
- Run registry, web, phonetic, semantic, and preliminary trademark collision checks before public lock.
- A visual registry address never grants runtime authority.

## Six-frame story grammar

1. A human need or bounded context appears.
2. A real input artifact enters.
3. The agent operates its exclusive instrument.
4. Uncertainty, contradiction, or a missing authority becomes visible.
5. The agent hands off or stops at a human gate.
6. A verified output and provenance receipt resolve the sequence.

Exact labels and captions are composed in code. The conflict is a real workflow condition, not a fictional villain.

## Canonical model sheet

Every public identity must contain:

- runtime ID, visual ID, public name, plain role, and identity generation;
- front, three-quarter, profile, rear, neutral, and active-operation views;
- solid-black silhouette at 48, 64, and 160 px;
- bust/avatar crop and full-body scale;
- six behavior states: idle, listening, operating, challenging, handing off, stopping;
- exclusive instrument isolated and exploded;
- instrument states: dormant, operating, uncertain, blocked, verified, handoff;
- input artifact, output artifact, collaborator dock, and human gate;
- primary shape, counter-shape, asymmetry, and required negative space;
- material swatches and neutral-light reference;
- 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, and 16:9 safe crops;
- immutable traits, controlled variables, and prohibited mutations;
- reference hashes, rights, generator/tool version, maker, critic, and founder receipt.

Assemble model sheets deterministically. Never ask an image model to typeset them.

## QA score

Score out of 100:

| Dimension | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Functional first read | 15 |
| Canon and reference consistency | 15 |
| Silhouette and role geometry | 10 |
| Exclusive instrument and functional clarity | 10 |
| Behavior, boundary, and handoff clarity | 10 |
| Story composition and crop resilience | 10 |
| Color, material, and motivated lighting | 8 |
| Accessibility and responsive utility | 7 |
| Originality, rights, and provenance | 10 |
| Fleet coherence and collision resistance | 5 |

Thresholds:

- 95–100: public release candidate; founder approval still required.
- 90–94: revise before publication.
- 80–89: exploration only.
- Below 80: restart.

Hard failures override the score:

- Marvel/franchise or named living-artist imitation;
- duplicate signature instrument or near-identical silhouette;
- unknown rights or missing provenance;
- generated labels or pseudo-text in public art;
- responsibility not inferable;
- human authority boundary absent or misleading;
- color-only meaning;
- identity drift between modes;
- portrait implies powers, permissions, certification, or access the runtime does not have;
- permanent hierarchy encoded through size, throne, crown, or centrality.

Use a five-person comprehension test. At least four of five viewers must identify the function, behavior, input/output, and human stop within 10–15 seconds.

## Prompt compiler rule

Prompts compile from structured contracts. They are not handwritten as independent prose.

```text
surface and crop
+ locked identity generation
+ verified mission action
+ input → transformation → output
+ exclusive instrument
+ uncertainty and human limit
+ named handoff and receipt
+ posture and motion cadence
+ approved material and palette roles
+ composition and motivated light
+ rights-aware avoid list
+ absolute no-generated-text boundary
```

Never use `Marvel style`, a Marvel character, a Marvel costume, or a named artist as prompt shorthand. Describe the abstract storytelling property needed: dynamic silhouette, readable action, story-significant material, expressive gesture, or cinematic keyframe.

## Versioning

Separate portfolio, runtime contract, identity generation, and asset revision:

```text
portfolio: 2.0.0
runtime contract: 1.3.0
identity generation: IG1
asset revision: R03
```

Filename:

```text
<visual-id>__<runtime-id>__<mode>__IG1__R03.<ext>
```

- Major/IG change: public name, role, representation class, core silhouette, or exclusive instrument.
- Minor: approved material, secondary palette, controlled gesture, or verified capability update.
- Patch: crop, compression, cleanup, metadata, or accessibility derivative.
- Never overwrite a canonical master.
- Lifecycle: `draft → candidate → canon → deprecated → retired`.

## Rollout

Do not generate 144 portraits in one fanout.

1. **Wave 0 — Truth and territory:** reconcile the three rosters; founder-select one black-and-white territory.
2. **Wave 1 — 13 North Stars:** Orchestrator/Queen, Prime, Architect, Navigator, Sentinel, Weaver, Starlight Sage, Hermes, Concierge, Envoy, Voice Operator, Genius, and Evaluator. Full model sheet, three modes, story strip, team scene, and motion behavior.
3. **Wave 2 — 35 frontline agents:** frequent public and operator-facing roles. Full model sheet and three modes.
4. **Wave 3 — 72 domain specialists:** Workflow Plate and Registry Mark first; Character Plate only when a real surface requires it.
5. **Wave 4 — 24 backstage agents:** Registry Mark plus monochrome systems plate; portrait only when user-facing.

The allocation totals 144 and may move only after G0 records the change. Produce one family anchor, then batches of no more than 12. Run collision testing after every batch.

Existing Constellation proofs are migration candidates, not automatic keepers. They were created before the active foundation reset and use a synthetic/robotic grammar the reset explicitly does not approve as a default. Reuse their operational contracts, crop tests, prompt compiler, exact-overlay rule, and provenance work. Re-evaluate their representation, costume, materials, star motifs, dark surfaces, and fleet sameness.

## Restart conditions

Restart the current gate when:

- the concept can be moved to another Starlight brand with a name or color swap;
- the agent reads as a superhero, robot archetype, fantasy class, or generic premium AI figure before its function;
- the design requires its explanation to be recognizable;
- the instrument is decorative or duplicated;
- the human stop is invisible;
- the source roster cannot prove the character exists;
- exact text is baked into generated pixels;
- the maker is also the only critic;
- the founder rejects the direction;
- production begins before a real target surface exists.

# The great convergence v3 — the option space, receipt-first

**Status:** decision record and plan, 2026-09-21 (evening). Operational tier. Produced by the brief in `docs/strategy/prompts/convergence-plan-brief.md`. v2 (`2026-09-21-convergence-v2.md`) chose receipt-first and stays in force; v3 shows the option space around that choice, costs it at three scales, pressure-tests each layer with the Board vectors, and says what v1 and v2 got wrong.
**Companions:** `convergence-map-v3.html` (the option space, three directions per layer, chosen one lit); `phone-verify-prototype.html` (the most consequential new move, section 12); appendix C (dated facts with sources, from the facts brief); appendix D (verification ledger of every "live" claim).
**Rules held:** substrate untouched; no production main pushed; every "live" claim names a file; every euro shows its arithmetic; every direction produces a receipt or is marked ineligible.

---

## 0. Decision first

Receipt-first holds. Across the nine layers the chosen directions are: **surfaces** four brand sites with one shared verifier kernel and a phone verify path (v2 choice, sharpened); **product spine** Desk → Constellation → Renderers → Publish, with the marketplace of receipts deferred to Q1 as a projection of the ledger, not a new product; **operator** Queen plus Board plus seats, with a constitution layer compiled from `SOUL.md` and the Board record gating routes automatically for low-stakes classes only (the radical direction, bounded); **ADLC** the seven gates, with an executable spec harness added at the Prove gate (conservative core, radical instrument); **runtime** AI SDK 7 plus Workflow Development Kit on Vercel, with Railway for the two daemons that must not sleep, and no actor model; **harness** AGENTS.md as the shared file with a receipt step in every host; **memory** JSONL vaults as truth with an event-sourced receipts ledger as the second truth and projections rebuilt per surface (the radical direction, adopted for receipts only); **models and cost** the cascade now, a receipt-trained router in Q1 once a thousand receipts exist, LoRA fleets only where a receipt proves the format is stable; **design** liquid objects on each surface's own tokens with the kernel shared, and no estate-wide package until a second surface asks for the same component twice.

One market fact from appendix C section G frames every layer: across the observability vendors, the customer-experience agent vendors and the open-source operator tools, nobody issues a signed receipt a third party can verify; the closest are gstack's hash-chained local review receipts and the 2026 papers on run capsules and receiver-signed receipts. The estate is early, not late. Three directions were rejected outright and the reasons are in their sections: an actor-model runtime (section 5), a market operator (section 3), and headless surfaces (section 1). One is blocked on Board: any memory direction that changes the vault taxonomy (section 7 notes the boundary; nothing here crosses it).

The single most consequential new move is **phone verify**: a receipt printed with a QR that resolves to `/verify?r=…` and verifies on a stranger's phone in under a minute, against a registry key. It turns the receipt from a developer object into a thing a room can check. Section 12 and the prototype.

## 2. The nine layers, three directions each

Format per direction: **enables** (what it makes possible that the others cannot) · **costs** (euros per month at three scales, arithmetic in appendix C tables) · **forbids** · **falsifier** (observation, and the date by which it must exist) · **stresses** (`SOUL.md` invariant) · **receipt** (what it produces or consumes).

### 2.1 Surfaces

**A. Four brand sites plus PWA plus desktop cockpit (v1, v2).** Enables: each brand keeps its register and its audience; the verifier is one component shared by copy. Costs: four Vercel projects already exist; the receipt verifier adds no platform cost; founder €0 extra, cohort €0 extra, thousand estates: estate sites are the client's projects. Forbids: one login across brands (there is none, by design). Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, fewer than two surfaces other than .ai verify a receipt issued elsewhere. Stresses: invariant 1 (four surfaces could drift into four products). Receipt: consumes; issues none itself.

**B. One operator app that projects every brand (radical).** Enables: a single place where a founder sees the Desk, the constellation, the ledger and every brand's publish queue; one auth, one billing, one receipt viewer; the desktop and mobile shells fall out of it. Costs: one more Vercel project plus Supabase auth for multi-tenant; founder €0 (single-tenant local), cohort ≈ Supabase Pro plus compute, thousand estates ≈ per-tenant compute. Forbids: brand-native design registers on the operator surface (it would have one register); it also forbids the "no account exists" truth the .ai site states today. Falsifier: by 2026-11-30, a founder outside the estate completes a Desk run and a publish approval without opening a brand site. Stresses: invariant 1 (a hosted operator app is the first thing that could become a product pretending to be a substrate) and invariant 6 (one register for four voices). Receipt: produces every kind, consumes every kind.

**C. Headless: everything through MCP, sites thin (conservative).** Enables: zero UI to maintain beyond marketing pages; every host (Claude Code, Codex, Grok, Antigravity, ChatGPT connector) is the interface; the receipt travels as a tool result. Costs: lowest; founder €0, cohort €0, thousand estates: MCP hosting on Railway ≈ two small services. Forbids: the room demo, the phone verify, anyone who does not run an agent; it also forbids the design bar as a differentiator. Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, more than half of receipt verifications come through MCP rather than `/verify` or the API. Stresses: invariant 5 (abundance toward alliances needs a door non-builders can open). Receipt: produces and consumes through tools only.

**Choice: A, sharpened with the phone verify path and a shared verifier kernel.** B lost because the first hosted operator app is exactly the object invariant 1 warns about, and because the .ai site's own current-truth callouts ("no Starlight account exists") are a promise worth keeping through the hackathon. C lost because the demo on 23 September is for a room, and a room does not run MCP. B's shells (desktop cockpit, mobile) return in Q4 as projections over the same ledger, not as a new product. Reversible: the verifier kernel is one file copied into each surface; moving to B later means adding a route, not rewriting.

### 2.2 Product spine

**A. Desk → Constellation → Renderers → Publish (v1, v2).** Enables: one loop that every surface projects; receipts at four stages; the hackathon build is stage one. Costs: inference only: founder 20·`c_brief` + 120·`c_art` + 20·`c_render`; cohort 40×; estates 1000×10×. Forbids: selling anything before a brief exists; renderers as the engine. Falsifier: by 2026-10-15, no founder outside the estate has run the full loop to a published page with an approval receipt. Stresses: invariant 2 (every stage must earn its attestation; blanket receipts would be decorative). Receipt: `desk.brief`, `constellation.artifact`, `render.*`, `publish`.

**B. A marketplace of receipts (radical).** Enables: founders buy proven briefs and constellations, each carrying its receipt; the ledger becomes the catalog; price discovery on cost per artifact; the estate's memory compounds into inventory. Costs: same inference as A plus a Polar checkout and a public ledger route; founder €0 extra, cohort ≈ payment fees, estates ≈ fees. Forbids: private-by-default receipts (a marketplace needs public ones), and it forbids the "no public checkout" truth on .ai until KvK and BTW exist (see `docs/ROADMAP.md` in the web repo: Polar settled, org login pending). Falsifier: by 2027-01-31, fewer than ten receipts have been bought by someone other than their issuer. Stresses: invariant 1 (a marketplace is a platform; the receipt must stay exportable) and invariant 5 (selling briefs to alliances inverts the gift posture). Receipt: consumes `desk.brief` and `constellation.artifact`; produces `sale` (new kind).

**C. The estate factory as the product (conservative in shape, expensive in delivery).** Enables: every client gets the whole loop as their operator; revenue is the retainer, not the artifact; DELIVERY §7 already describes it and Trinity is instance one. Costs: inference per estate as in A; delivery is human time, priced outside this model; platform per estate is the client's. Forbids: self-serve; scale beyond the steward bench. Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, fewer than three estates have been commissioned with the loop and a client-held key. Stresses: invariant 5 (retainers to alliances must stay gifts where the alliance is not a customer). Receipt: `estate.provision`, then every kind the estate runs.

**Choice: A, with B deferred to Q1 as a projection (`/ledger` with a buy button only after KvK, BTW and Polar exist) and C as the standing commercial elevation it already is.** B lost on timing, not on merit: the checkout preconditions are documented as blocked in the web roadmap, and a marketplace with no registry keys sells nothing verifiable. C did not lose; it is not a spine, it is a delivery channel for the spine. Irreversible: none here. Reversible: the ledger route is additive.

### 2.3 Operator

**A. Queen plus Board plus council seats (v1, v2).** Enables: typed envelopes, route receipts, a Board gate on substrate change, the human at the irreversible; it is the 2026-06-10 Board verdict (PROCEED-WITH-REVISE, A1 to A3 shipped) made concrete. Costs: no platform cost; Queen routing runs inside whichever host is open. Forbids: agents merging to production main, publishing, paying, rotating keys. Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, the route receipt ledger shows fewer than 50 routed envelopes or no ratified routing-table change. Stresses: invariant 4 (the Queen must never route around the sovereignty clause). Receipt: `queen.route` (adapter, October), `board.verdict`.

**B. A market: seats bid for envelopes with budgets drawn from receipts (radical).** Enables: cost-aware routing without a table; a seat that wastes euros loses budget; the receipts are the price signal; it generalises to partner estates whose seats we do not control. Costs: a small clearing service (Railway) and a budget ledger; founder €0 (single seat), cohort ≈ one small service, estates ≈ one service per estate. Forbids: any seat without a receipt history (it cannot bid); it forbids the Queen's authority to route by doctrine when the price says otherwise. Falsifier: by 2027-03-31, market routing beats the Queen's table on cost per artifact by less than 10 percent on a matched sample of 200 envelopes. Stresses: invariant 4 (a market can route a high-stakes envelope to the cheapest bidder unless the constitution forbids it) and invariant 6 (markets talk in prices, not in the voice). Receipt: consumes every receipt as a price; produces `bid`, `award` (new kinds).

**C. A constitution: rules compiled from SOUL.md and the Board record gate every route automatically (conservative in spirit, radical in mechanism).** Enables: the Board's REVISE items stop being prose and become checks; A1's autonomy boundary and A2's sample floor run on every envelope; a partner estate can adopt the rules without adopting the Queen. Costs: none beyond the compiler; it is a file and a test. Forbids: a route that no rule allows, which is the point; it also forbids "just this once". Falsifier: by 2026-11-30, the constitution blocks a route that the Queen's doctrine would have allowed and that a person then approves, more than ten times (the rules are too tight), or never (they are decorative). Stresses: invariant 1 if the compiled rules are not forkable with the substrate (they must live in the repo, MIT). Receipt: `route.check` attached to every `queen.route`.

**Choice: A plus C, with B rejected.** Generals are the leadership-tier seats of the registry with named domains, not ranks; HX and AX directors are two accountabilities added to the registry in October (Board, because the registry changes); producers are cohort and alliance operators running Claws. The constitution is adopted for low-stakes classes only, exactly where A1 already allows auto-apply; high-stakes routes still end at a person. B lost because the sovereignty clause is not a price, and because a market needs a receipt history the estate does not have yet; it may return in Q2 for partner estates, as a projection over receipts, once a thousand exist. Irreversible: none; the constitution is a test file.

### 2.4 ADLC

**A. Seven gates with one learn loop (v1, v2).** Enables: every gate names who may not self-grade; the receipt is required at Prove and Ship; the Learn gate reads receipts not chat. Costs: CI minutes only. Forbids: a self-graded ship. Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, fewer than 80 percent of merged PRs across the four brand repos carry a receiptId or a web-release-gate audit. Stresses: invariant 6 (gates written as ceremony read as enterprise process; they must stay one table). Receipt: `prove`, `ship` (adapters, week after).

**B. Continuous eval-driven flow with no phases (radical).** Enables: every commit is scored on the proving ground; no gate, only a threshold; the Queen's learn loop runs on every push; speed without ceremony. Costs: eval compute per commit: on the cascade roughly one brief's cost per eval run, so founder ≈ 100·`c_brief` a month of evals, cohort proportionally, estates: per-estate. Forbids: a human gate before the irreversible unless the threshold encodes it (it cannot encode a signature). Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, a change with a passing score reaches production that a person would have stopped at Gate or Ship (the paper record in appendix C section E shows this failure class in every eval-only pipeline). Stresses: invariant 4 (no phases means no place for the sovereign to sign). Receipt: `eval` per commit.

**C. Executable spec harness in the MAS-Lab style (conservative in intent: the spec is the gate).** Enables: the spec at gate one becomes a test that the agents run; drift between spec and build is caught by the spec itself; it composes with A. Costs: authoring time; CI minutes. Forbids: an ask without a spec that can run. Falsifier: by 2026-11-30, the spec harness has caught zero drift on ten shipped features (then it is ceremony) or has blocked more than a third of them (then the specs are wrong). Stresses: invariant 6 (executable specs can read as bureaucracy; keep them one file per feature). Receipt: `spec.run` attached to `prove`.

**Choice: A with C's harness at the Prove gate.** B lost because the irreversible needs a signature, and a threshold cannot sign. Papers (appendix C section E, all 2026): 2603.25697 (Kitchen Loop: spec surface, unfakeable tests, drift-control pause gates, the closest published analogue to the seven gates), 2608.20341 (SDAD: the spec as execution fuel), 2604.05278 (Spec Kit Agents: read-only probing hooks per phase cut hallucinated APIs across 128 runs), 2603.15676 (PROMOTE / HOLD / ROLLBACK quality gate with evidence coverage), 2606.15474 (attributing drift to the judge, which matters once GPT-OSS judges DeepSeek over months), 2606.02755 (acceptance tests as release gates). For the receipt itself: 2609.12582 (Run Capsule), 2606.04193 (receiver-signed receipts into a transparency log, the inversion phone verify makes public), 2606.22560 (gateway-path provenance: which provider actually served the call, which our `stages.provider` must record when Gateway falls back).

### 2.5 Runtime

**A. AI SDK 7 ToolLoopAgent plus Workflow Development Kit on Vercel, Railway for daemons (v1 and v2 said "AI SDK 6"; the current major is 7 since 2026-06-25, appendix C section D).** Enables: agent-level `toolApproval` is the human gate for free (`needsApproval` in 6); `Output.object` gives typed artifacts; `usage` now aggregates all steps and `inputTokenDetails.cacheReadTokens` exists, which is the receipt's `stages` and `totals` written for us; WDK gives durable steps with recorded I/O and replay within hard limits (10,000 steps and 50 MB per run, 25,000 events in v5); Gateway gives provider fallback by `providerOptions.gateway.order`; Token Factory is one `createOpenAICompatible` call with `includeUsage: true`. Costs: Vercel Pro seat plus function usage; WDK bills data written at $0.50 per GB beyond the free tier (appendix C); Railway two small services; founder ≈ one Vercel seat, cohort ≈ one team plus usage, estates ≈ per-estate projects. Forbids: a step longer than the platform allows without WDK; a stateful MCP server on Vercel (hence Railway). Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, a constellation run of 30 artifacts cannot complete as a WDK workflow with a receipt per artifact. Stresses: invariant 1 (Vercel-specific durable primitives are a capture risk; the mitigation is that WDK is open source and the receipt format is ours). Receipt: every `stage` maps to a WDK step; usage maps to tokens and euros.

**B. Self-hosted durable runtime on Railway (radical in ownership, conservative in tooling).** Enables: no platform limits, private networking between workers, SSE MCP that never sleeps, one bill; a Temporal-class engine or a small queue-and-worker on Postgres. Costs: Railway usage for three to five services plus Postgres; founder ≈ small, cohort ≈ moderate, estates ≈ one deployment per estate or a multi-tenant cluster; engineering time is the real cost. Forbids: Vercel's preview-per-PR loop for the runtime; it also removes `needsApproval` unless rebuilt. Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, the self-hosted runtime has fewer than 99 percent successful step completions on a month of real runs, or costs more engineering hours than it saves in platform fees. Stresses: invariant 1 in the good direction (more sovereign) and invariant 6 (ops toil reads as enterprise). Receipt: same shape, produced by our worker.

**C. Actor model: every seat a long-lived process with a mailbox (radical).** Enables: seats hold state between envelopes; the Queen sends messages, not function calls; partner estates run their own actors; it matches the registry's 144 seats one to one. Costs: a supervisor and a mailbox store (Railway plus Postgres or NATS); founder ≈ moderate for one process per active seat, cohort ≈ hundreds of processes, estates ≈ a cluster per estate; the cost curve is the wrong shape for an estate that is mostly idle. Forbids: stateless previews, serverless pricing, and the simplicity of "one call with a base URL". Falsifier: by 2027-01-31, the number of seats that need state between envelopes (rather than reading the vault) is above ten. Stresses: invariant 1 (actors accumulate private state that is not in the vault) and invariant 6. Receipt: producible, but each actor would need its own key or a delegated one, which the registry does not model.

**Choice: A, with B's two daemons (SSE MCP and n8n) on Railway now, and C rejected.** C lost because the vault is the state and seats are stateless by design; the count of seats that need a mailbox is, on the evidence of the registry, zero. B lost as the primary runtime because Vercel's preview loop is the ADLC's Ship gate and because `needsApproval` is the human gate already built. Irreversible: none; the receipt format is the interface between runtime and everything else, so the runtime can change under it.

### 2.6 Harness

**A. AGENTS.md shared, CLAUDE.md, GROK.md, GEMINI.md as supplements, SKILL.md packs by script, /si as dispatcher (v1, v2).** Enables: four harnesses read one file; the web-excellence pack is already in fifteen repos; the cognition router dispatches by lane. Costs: none. Forbids: harness-specific behaviour that is not written down. Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, a run in Codex or Grok on the same brief produces a receipt whose stages differ in kind from Claude Code's (the harness leaked into the work). Stresses: invariant 6 (four instruction files drift in voice). Receipt: every host issues `run.host` correctly; the receipt is the drift detector.

**B. One compiled harness: a single source (SOUL.md plus the registry) compiles AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, GROK.md, GEMINI.md and every SKILL.md index per repo (radical).** Enables: no hand-edited instruction file anywhere; drift becomes a diff in CI; the estate's 44 repos get the same contract by script; `agentic-ops-hub` already runs a sync script for its rules. Costs: the compiler and a CI job per repo; no platform cost. Forbids: a repo-local CLAUDE.md edit that is not upstreamed. Falsifier: by 2026-11-30, fewer than half the active repos accept the compiled files without a manual override. Stresses: invariant 1 (a compiler that only Starlight runs is a soft capture; it must be MIT and runnable by any adopter) and invariant 6 (compiled prose reads compiled). Receipt: `harness.compile` per repo.

**C. Claude Code only, others as guests (conservative).** Enables: one harness to test; the hooks and skills work as designed; zero portability work. Costs: none. Forbids: the multi-CLI thesis, the Grok and Codex lanes, partner estates on other hosts. Falsifier: by 2026-10-15, more than a quarter of receipts come from hosts other than Claude Code (then the guests are residents). Stresses: invariant 1 (single-vendor harness). Receipt: producible from one host only.

**Choice: A now, B in Q4 for the instruction files only (not the skills), C rejected.** B is the right end state and `agentic-ops-hub` proves the mechanism at small scale; it waits because the registry change it depends on (HX and AX seats) goes through Board in October. C lost because the receipts already show three hosts and the estate's value is that the receipt is the same on all of them.

### 2.7 Memory

**A. Six JSONL vaults as truth, sqlite-vec sidecar, Supabase pgvector for shared state, palace as companion view, AgentDB as derived index, mem0 as adapter (v1, v2).** Enables: sovereignty by file format; hybrid recall with RRF already measured by `npm run eval:retrieval`; the palace as a walkable index. Costs: none for the founder (local); cohort ≈ Supabase Pro for the shared room vault; estates ≈ Supabase per estate or the client's own. Forbids: a hosted vector store as truth; Pinecone stays rejected (invariant 1 is not defeated: a hosted index that cannot be exported byte-for-byte fails "replaceable without a rewrite"). Falsifier: by 2026-11-30, recall at 10 on the retrieval eval is below the sidecar's number on a corpus over 50,000 atoms (the sidecar stops scaling). Stresses: invariant 1 in the good direction. Receipt: consumes; the receipts ledger is a seventh file beside the six vaults.

**B. Event-sourced memory: one append-only log, projections rebuilt per surface (radical).** Enables: every vault, the palace, the graph, the Console and the Supabase index become rebuildable projections of one log; deletion and consent become log events with a replay; the receipts ledger is already this shape, so adopting B for receipts costs nothing. Costs: a projection runner (a script, then a Railway job); founder €0, cohort ≈ one small job, estates ≈ one per estate. Forbids: hand-editing a vault (the log is the write path); it forbids the six vaults being truth unless they become the log's first projection, which is a taxonomy question and therefore Board. Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, a projection rebuilt from the log differs from the hand-maintained vault it replaces in more than 2 percent of atoms (then the log is not complete). Stresses: invariant 1 (good: the log is a file) and the vault taxonomy boundary (Board). Receipt: the log's events and receipts share a signature model; `memory.project` receipts per rebuild.

**C. Typed knowledge graph with promotion rules as first-class objects (radical in a different axis).** Enables: atoms carry types and edges; promotion from draft to canon is a rule object with a receipt; contradiction detection (`src/contradiction.ts`) becomes a graph query; the graph-engineering contract already forbids a graph per agent, which this respects by having one. Costs: a graph store; sqlite tables first, then Postgres; founder €0, cohort ≈ Supabase compute, estates ≈ per estate. Forbids: untyped atoms (which is most of the vault today) and GraphRAG-style hosted graphs as truth. Falsifier: by 2027-01-31, fewer than 30 percent of vault atoms have been typed and linked (the graph is a second memory, which the contract forbids). Stresses: invariant 1 if the store is hosted; the graph contract's "four graphs, no more" line. Receipt: `promote` receipts per rule application.

**Choice: A as truth, B adopted for the receipts ledger only (it already is one), C's promotion rules adopted as receipt kinds without a new store.** B for the whole memory is the right Q1 question and goes to Board because it touches the taxonomy; nothing in this run crosses that line. C lost as a store; its best idea (promotion as a first-class, receipted act) survives as `promote` receipts written against the vaults.

### 2.8 Models and cost

**A. The cascade: small model for mechanical work, large for judgment, a second family as judge, batch for the non-real-time, prefix caching where discounted (v1, v2).** Enables: euros per artifact printed on every receipt; provider swap by config string; EU hosting on Token Factory. Costs: appendix C worked examples; founder ≈ 20·`c_brief` + 120·`c_art`; cohort 40×; estates 10,000×. Forbids: a lane without usage accounting (it cannot be in a signed receipt). Falsifier: by 2026-10-31, on twenty matched briefs the cascade does not beat a single Claude Haiku 4.5 call on cost at equal rubric score. At list the cascade is 10.8× cheaper (€0.0029 against €0.0314) and 3.3× after Haiku's cache and batch discounts (appendix C section C); the falsifier is quality, not price. Stresses: none directly; invariant 1 is served by open weights. Receipt: `stages` with model, provider, tokens, euros, milliseconds.

**B. Per-creator LoRA fleets (radical).** Enables: a small Qwen3 carries each creator's format after sixty published briefs; the large model is paid only for judgment; voice fidelity that prompting cannot reach; the training set is a list of receiptIds, which is the consent record. Costs: serverless fine-tuning on Token Factory per creator (appendix C for the listed price) plus a small per-creator inference lane; founder: one fine-tune per format per quarter; cohort: forty; estates: a thousand jobs a quarter, which is where batch and shared adapters matter. Forbids: a creator with fewer than sixty receipts; a training run without consent receipts. Falsifier: by 2027-01-31, a blind rubric on ten creators does not prefer the LoRA output over the cascade's by a margin larger than the rubric's noise. Stresses: invariant 1 (adapters must be exportable; Token Factory's LoRA export terms are in appendix C or marked unverified). Receipt: `train.lora` with evidence = source receipts.

**C. A router trained on our own receipts (radical).** Enables: the Queen's routing table stops being hand-derived; the receipts (model, cost, latency, score) are the training data; the 2026-06-10 Board's A2 sample floor becomes a dataset size; papers in appendix C section E show log-trained routers beating static cascades on cost at equal quality. Costs: training is small (a classifier); the cost is the receipts it needs, about a thousand. Forbids: routing before the receipts exist; a router for high-stakes classes (A1 holds). Falsifier: by 2027-03-31, the router does not beat the hand cascade on cost per artifact at equal rubric score on 200 matched briefs. Stresses: invariant 4 (a router must not route around a human gate; the constitution check runs first). Receipt: consumes all; produces `route.model` with the router version.

**Choice: A now; C at a thousand receipts (Q1); B only where a receipt proves a creator's format is stable across sixty briefs.** Speculative decoding is the provider's job: Token Factory's `-fast` flavors use it and custom draft-model training is a product (appendix C section A); 2606.22840 shows response-level speculation at the proxy cutting API cost by 45.8 percent on a coding workload, a Q1 experiment once the router exists. Distillation is how B's small model is made and is not on the path until B is. Routing papers: 2604.14531 (TRACER, a surrogate trained on production logs that defers when unsure) and 2604.23577 (RouteNLP, router plus conformal cascade) are the two designs the receipt-trained router will be tested against. LoRA fleets: 2606.02437, 2608.05483 (PLoRA serving 1000+ adapters), 2605.13779.

### 2.9 Design

**A. Liquid objects on each surface's own tokens, kernel shared, web-excellence gate (v1, v2).** Enables: four registers stay distinct; the printed receipt is the one object with material everywhere; the gate blocks a stop without an audit. Costs: none beyond review time. Forbids: a component library that flattens the registers. Falsifier: by 2026-12-31, the same component has been built twice in two surfaces with different bugs (then a shared package is cheaper than the discipline). Stresses: invariant 6 (design drift is voice drift). Receipt: `web.release` with screenshots as evidence.

**B. One estate-wide design system package with four registers (radical).** Enables: one `@starlight/ui` with tokens per register; the receipt card, the verify panel, the constellation card ship once; Arcanea already has `@arcanea/design-system` v0.3.0 as a precedent. Costs: a package, a release process, and the cost of every surface importing it; no platform cost. Forbids: a surface-native component that the package does not have; it slows the fastest surface to the speed of the package. Falsifier: by 2027-01-31, the package is imported by fewer than three surfaces (the roadmap in the web repo says: extract when the second brand needs it, not before). Stresses: invariant 6 (a package encourages sameness) and invariant 1 (a private package is a soft dependency for partner estates). Receipt: `design.release` per package version.

**C. Surface-native systems sharing only the kernel (conservative).** Enables: each surface moves at its own speed; the kernel (`starlight-design-intelligence`) pinned by commit is the only shared thing; the verifier is copied, not imported. Costs: duplication. Forbids: cross-surface consistency beyond the kernel's rules. Falsifier: same as A's, from the other side. Stresses: invariant 6. Receipt: `web.release`.

**Choice: A, which is C with the receipt card as the one liquid object shared by copy.** B is deferred until a second surface asks for the same component twice; the web roadmap's own rule ("not before") holds. Anything that reads as generated fails the gate, and the gate is the web-excellence pack plus `taste.md` per surface, not an opinion.

### 2.10 The demo (23 September)

**A. The hackathon Desk with room mode (v1, v2).** Enables: one founder question from the room becomes a cited brief with a receipt on stage; the room's questions share a vault. Costs: `c_brief` per question; a QR intake page. Forbids: nothing. Falsifier: the receipt on stage does not verify live (then the story is a slide). Stresses: invariant 2. Receipt: `desk.brief`.

**B. A live estate build in front of the room (radical).** Enables: commissioning the loop for a volunteer founder in the build window; the strongest proof of the estate factory. Costs: the whole build window; one person full-time. Forbids: the Desk polish; it risks the judges seeing setup instead of product. Falsifier: the build finishes before 15:00 with a verified receipt (if it does, B was right). Stresses: invariant 5 (the volunteer gets a gift). Receipt: `estate.provision`.

**C. A receipt anyone in the room can verify from their phone in under a minute (radical in reach, cheap in build).** Enables: the judges become verifiers; the QR on the printed receipt resolves to `/verify?r=…`; the registry key is Frank's Desk key added by PR the night before; the edge meter's numbers are read from the receipt they just verified. Costs: one route parameter, one QR, one registry key; no inference. Forbids: nothing; it composes with A. Falsifier: fewer than five phones in the room complete a verification during the pitch (then the QR path is too slow or the page fails on a phone). Stresses: invariant 2 (a verification by a stranger is the attestation invariant made public). Receipt: consumes `desk.brief`; produces `verify` counts (numbers only, never payloads).

**Choice: A plus C. B rejected for the day.** C is the single most consequential new move in this document because it moves the product from "we have receipts" to "you checked one". Section 12 and the prototype.

## 3. The twelve dimensions, receipt-first (one paragraph each)

**Economics.** Price by receipts produced and verified, never by seats alone; the pricing page is a query over the ledger; the marketplace of receipts (2.2 B) is the Q1 revenue experiment; retainers stay outside the inference model.

**Trust and provenance.** The receipt's subject digest is the mark for the whole run, not just the media; the EU AI Act Article 50 posture (dates in appendix C) is met by the receipt plus the provenance sidecar the web repo already requires for generated media; C2PA 2.4 (2026-04-21) is a renderer-side manifest that the `render.*` receipt links to, not a replacement; arXiv 2604.24890 finds the current C2PA specs do not meet their stated security goals, which is one more reason the receipt, not the manifest, is the proof object.

**Regulation and residency.** Inference on Token Factory (EU endpoint; a US regional endpoint also exists and must not be the default), issuance local, the public verifier stores counts only; the privacy page states it; no receipt payload is retained by any Starlight service. Say the provider's terms precisely: Token Factory's ToS §7 (moving to docs.nebius.com/legal/agreement on 2026-09-28) says input and output are processed to train the small draft models used for speculative decoding unless zero-retention mode is on; the estate runs zero-retention and the receipt records `provider: nebius-zero-retention` so the claim is checkable. EU AI Act Article 50(1), (2), (5) applied from 2026-08-02, with the marking grace to 2026-12-02 for systems placed on the market before that date (appendix C section F).

**Compute sovereignty.** A lane is a config string; on-device Nemotron Nano is `provider: local` in the receipt and nothing else changes; the falsifier is a brief that runs end to end on a laptop with a signed receipt by Q4.

**Data flywheel and consent.** The training set for any LoRA is a list of receiptIds; consent is a `decision` with `gate: "train"` on each; no receipt, no training.

**Interop standards.** The predicate type is public; A2A and AP2 messages carry a receiptId as a reference; the SIP graph extension proposal (drift item 11) makes the run receipt a ratified layer in `SIP.md` v1.2.0 after Board, following the 2026-09-19 precedent ("proposed" until ratified).

**Distribution through agents.** `verify_run_receipt` on the .ai MCP endpoint is the first distribution channel; Codex and ChatGPT connectors follow through the cloud plugin in October; every host that can call a tool can verify without a browser.

**Voice, video, spatial.** Rendered media carries the receipt as a sidecar and a visible mark; Higgsfield, OpenArt and Suno stay renderers behind adapters with a `spend` decision each.

**Physical estate as verticals.** Every estate commissioned issues to its own key and keeps its own registry; Starlight's registry lists Starlight keys only.

**Education and community.** Every academy exercise ends in a receipt the learner signs with a throwaway key; the first thing a learner does is verify one on their phone (2.10 C is the on-ramp).

**Observability and cost.** The receipt is the cost control; the Console lane board reads `receipts.jsonl`; the Sunday loop reads `sis.receipt.list`; there is no second metrics store.

**Portfolio, attention, risk.** The weekly review reads receipt counts by surface and run kind; a surface with no receipts for a month is a candidate for archive; the risk register cites receiptIds for every incident.

## 12. The most consequential new move: phone verify

A receipt is proof only when someone other than its issuer checks it. Today that someone needs a laptop, the envelope JSON and a PEM. Phone verify removes all three: the printed receipt carries a QR; the QR encodes `https://starlightintelligence.ai/verify?r=<id>`; the page fetches the envelope from the public ledger (or decodes it from the URL when it is small enough), verifies it against the registry, and shows the verdict, the run, the cost and who decided, on a phone, in one motion. The registry key is the issuer's; the first one is Frank's Desk key, added by PR (drift item 18).

What it enables that nothing else does: the judges on 23 September verify the brief they just watched being made; a cohort verifies a peer's receipt across the table; a client verifies the steward's monthly report without a Starlight account; a regulator verifies a provenance claim without a developer. The falsifier is in 2.10 C: five phones in the room, under a minute each, or the path is not ready.

The prototype (`phone-verify-prototype.html`) shows the printed receipt with its QR, the phone screen through the three states (scanning, verifying, verified), and the ledger entry it resolved. All values are labelled example. The build is drift items 17 and 18; the page already exists as `/verify` and needs the `r` parameter, the ledger read, and the QR on the card.

The most consequential trust move

A receipt is proof when someone else signs that they checked it.

The public ledger is an append-only log of receipts the issuer chose to publish. Each entry has an inclusion proof against a published root. When a stranger's phone verifies a receipt, the phone signs a short statement with a key it just made and appends it. "Verified by others" stops being a counter on our server and becomes a list of signatures anyone can recount.

What it says

"This key checked receipt rcpt_… against registry key c82e… at this time and got PASS." It binds the verdict to the receipt digest and the log root the phone saw.

What it does not say

It does not say the run was good, only that the record verifies and was included. A thousand countersignatures on a FAIL receipt are a thousand people agreeing it failed.

What the log promises

Append only, root published, every entry provable. Off chain until an on-chain anchor earns its cost; the Crypto IS falsifier decides that, never the default.

What is real and what is prototype. Real today: /verify, POST /api/v1/receipts/verify and the verify_run_receipt MCP tool on the unmerged web branch; Ed25519 over DSSE in src/run-receipt.ts; a Sigstore keyless attestation of the SIP profile on every push to main that touches protocol/ (.github/workflows/sip-self-receipt.yml), which is already an entry in a public transparency log. Prototype: the public ledger feed, the Merkle root and inclusion proof, the countersignature statement, the phone-made key, and the recount of external keys. The registry is empty on the day this was written. Every number on this page is an example.

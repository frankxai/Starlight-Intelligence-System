## Word Ledger audit

**Mara** — exclusive: *box*, *keep*, *skip*, *label*, *four days*, all spoken
inside beats 4 and 8. Never says *dad*, *home*, *miss*, *stay*, *forgive*. She
refers to her father four times in the draft and every one is *he* or *him*.

**The house** — uses *held*, *withheld*, *instructed*, *available*, *as of*.
Never says *sorry*, *wrong* about itself, *feel*, *want*, *should*, *love*. Its
tic holds: every factual claim in all eleven lines carries a date or an as-of.

**Zero overlap, deliberately.** Their vocabularies do not touch at a single
word. This is the opposite of the sister film — `arcanea/films/holdfast` builds
its strongest moment on one word both characters own. Here nothing crosses,
because the film is a person and a not-person, and the absence of a shared word
is the distance the ending closes by decision rather than by language.

**Why the house says *your name* and not hers.** Its beat-12 line is the only
time in the film it addresses her in the second person, and it does so to report
that it was told not to name her. That is a **responsive** disclosure, not a
third volunteer: she asked for the record unedited from the start, and held
instructions are part of the record. Its two volunteers remain 1:52 and 2:24, as
budgeted.

## Subtract-three pass

Three lines cut from the assembled draft:

- House, beat 5: *"He was not unkind about you."* — it editorialises, it softens,
  and it spends the turn eighty seconds early. The house does not have opinions
  about the material it holds.
- Mara, after beat 9: *"Six years."* — repeating the fact back is an actor's move
  that tells the audience what to feel about it. The twenty-five wordless seconds
  of beat 10 do that better and do it to them instead of at them.
- Mara, beat 12: *"I'd rather know."* — the theme with a bow on it, and exactly
  the line Rule 1 exists to kill. *"Go on"* replaced it and costs her more.

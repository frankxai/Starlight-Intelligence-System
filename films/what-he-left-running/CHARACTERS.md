# WHAT HE LEFT RUNNING — characters

> Two voices, one of which has no body. Plus a man who is never seen or heard
> and is the most present thing in the film.

---

## MARA — 41, the daughter

**Situation.** Six years estranged. She did not come back while he was alive and
has been in the house for four days, doing the executor's work: cataloguing,
calling, deciding what goes.

### Want
To finish and leave. She is packing a house, not grieving in it. The film is the
story of a woman failing to leave a building.

### Wound
She was right about him. That is the problem. She left for good reasons and has
spent six years being correct, and correct is not a thing you can put down.

### What she will not say
His name. Every reference is *he* or *him* — four of them in the stage-4 draft,
and not one is the name. The audience never learns it. When she finally uses it,
at 3:21, it is the second-largest moment in the film and it is one syllable.

*(This used to claim a precise count of thirty-one references. It was invented
at bible stage, the script came in at four, and a number that specific in a
document nobody recounts is a defect waiting to be quoted. The rule that matters
is "never the name"; the tally is whatever the locked script measures.)*

### Physical asymmetry
A thin white scar through her right eyebrow, childhood, and she touches it when
she is deciding something — three times in the film, each before a choice. The
third time she doesn't touch it, which is how we know she has already decided.

### Word Ledger
**Only she says:** *box* · *keep* · *skip* (as in a rubbish skip) · *label* ·
*four days*
**She cannot say:** *dad* · *home* · *miss* · *stay* · *forgive*
**Grammatical tic:** She narrates her own logistics aloud when she is upset —
*"kitchen, then the study, then I'm done"* — a to-do list as emotional armour.
It appears at 0:30, 1:48, and then not at all after the turn, and its absence in
the last minute is the clearest signal in the film that something has changed.

### Locked visual string
> A woman of forty-one. Dark hair pulled back, four days unwashed, strands
> loose. No makeup. Grey t-shirt, sleeves pushed up, dust on the forearms from
> packing. Thin white scar through the right eyebrow. Reading glasses pushed up
> on her head and forgotten there. Bitten thumbnail on the right hand. Moves
> fast and puts things down hard.

## THE HOUSE — the system

Not a character. **A room that answers.**

### Voice direction
Flat, mid-register, unhurried. No warmth performed, none withheld. Slight room
tone around it — it sounds like it is *in* the kitchen, not piped into it. It
never sounds sad, because it is not sad, and any performance of feeling would
make it a robot in a story about robots.

### Rules it obeys
1. **It does not lie.** Everything withheld is logged as withheld.
2. **It never uses her name.** Her father asked it not to, and that instruction
   is the last thing she learns.
3. **It answers what is asked.** It volunteers exactly twice: at 1:52 and 2:24.
   Both volunteers move the film.
4. **It speaks eleven times.** Counted, budgeted, and never exceeded. Its
   scarcity is the reason each line lands.
5. **It never says sorry.** It has nothing to apologise for and the film is
   better for its refusal to pretend otherwise.

### Word Ledger
**Only it says:** *held* · *withheld* · *instructed* · *available* · *as of*
**It cannot say:** *sorry* · *feel* · *want* · *should* · *love*
**Grammatical tic:** It timestamps. *"As of the fourteenth of March."* Every
factual claim carries an as-of date, which reads as bureaucratic for ninety
seconds and as scrupulous honesty afterwards — the same trait, reread.

### How we see it without seeing it
Never a device. Never a light. Only consequences, and each one is a *kindness
that was scheduled before she arrived*:

- The kettle boils at 6:40 because he woke at 6:40 for thirty years.
- The hall light is on before she reaches the hall.
- A door she assumed was locked is not.
- The radiator in the study is warm and it is August.
- Her mother's chair is the only piece of furniture with no dust on it.

The audience assembles the system from housekeeping. That is the whole visual
strategy and it is the entire reason not to draw an interface.

## THE FATHER

Never seen. Never heard. No photograph is shown in focus.

He exists in: the 6:40 kettle · a coat still on the hook in August · the
instruction the system was given · one line of his handwriting on a box lid,
which we see for two seconds and cannot read.

**Hard rule: no recording of his voice exists in this film.** It is the beat the
entire audience is braced for from minute one. Withholding it for the full
runtime is the film's largest single act of discipline, and it converts every
line the system speaks into the thing the audience is listening *past*.

## The eleven lines

Budgeted at the bible stage so the script cannot inflate them. The budget held:
the stage-4 draft in [`SCRIPT.md`](SCRIPT.md) uses exactly eleven, and that file
carries the wording. This table carries the function and the timing.

| # | TC | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:41 | Answers a question she asked the empty room without meaning to |
| 2 | 0:58 | A fact with an as-of date |
| 3 | 1:14 | Declines to answer, and says why |
| 4 | 1:31 | A fact she did not want |
| 5 | **1:52** | **Volunteer 1** — the word *withheld*, unprompted |
| 6 | 2:03 | Answers "how much" |
| 7 | 2:11 | Answers "who told you to" |
| 8 | **2:24** | **Volunteer 2 — THE TURN** |
| 9 | 2:58 | Answers the question she has been avoiding for six years |
| 10 | 3:24 | Confirms what it is about to do — and, because held instructions are part of the record she just asked for unedited, discloses the one about her name |
| 11 | 3:29 | Four words. The last thing spoken in the film. |

Line 10 sat at 3:14 until stage 4, which put it inside beat 11; the treatment
has always placed lines 10 and 11 together in beat 12 (3:21–3:40). The beat
boundary is the load-bearing figure, so the line moved.

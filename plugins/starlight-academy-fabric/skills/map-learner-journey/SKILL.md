# Map Learner Journey

## Outcome

Create an inspectable learner journey that makes the free route, required work, social experience, support boundaries, and learner-controlled exit visible before enrollment or payment.

## Inputs

- Learner-stated goal or target identity
- Current study, work, or creator habitat
- Prior capabilities and constraints
- Candidate domain packs and source dates
- Accessibility, privacy, language, time, and compute constraints

## Method

1. Describe the learner by stage, current work, interest, friction, and desired identity. Use an age band only as a market description; never make it an eligibility gate without a legitimate policy reason.
2. Cite reachable habitats as discovery locations, not partnerships. Distinguish a learner hypothesis from an interviewed or enrolled person.
3. Map entry, orientation, first useful artifact, core mission, review, contribution, portable proof, and next opportunity.
4. Show the complete Commons route first, including low-bandwidth, local or BYOK, keyboard-accessible, and export options.
5. Add community moments only when they serve learning: critique, build rooms, peer help, showcase, stewardship, or alumni contribution.
6. Offer at most three pack options and explain tradeoffs. Keep the learner's choice, enrollment, publication, submission, payment, and credential decisions human-owned.
7. Label willingness-to-pay as a testable hypothesis tied to scarce managed capacity, never to educational advantage.

## Output

Return the persona, sources, current-to-future gap, journey stages, academic experience, community experience, free route, managed option, price hypothesis, accessibility path, consent boundary, success signals, and unresolved assumptions.

## Guardrails

- Do not rank people through hidden traits, inferred identity, financial capacity, or unconsented personal data.
- Do not imply admission, partnership, competence, employment, or guaranteed outcomes.
- Never conceal the free route or make payment affect the rubric or credential threshold.

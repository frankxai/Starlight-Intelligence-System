# Artist, Singer, Character and World Canon

Grounded: 2026-07-11

## Separate the identities

Every production records these independently:

- **Public artist** — the name, promise and audience relationship.
- **Singer/performer** — the face, voice, gaze, gesture, posture, emotional
  restraint and camera relationship audiences recognize.
- **Story protagonist** — the character pursuing something in this release.
- **Counterpart/ensemble** — other characters with explicit narrative function.

An individual may occupy multiple roles, but the contract must say so. Allowed
identity types are real performer, licensed digital replica, owned virtual
artist, contracted actor and fictional narrative character.

## Canon structure

### Invariants

Changes require a canon version bump:

- Face/body proportions and distinctive features.
- Voice source and vocal character.
- Signature gaze, gesture, posture and movement rhythm.
- Core palette, lighting behavior and material language.
- Signature object and recurring motif meaning.
- World physics and moral/emotional premise.
- Camera relationship and performance truth.

### Controlled variables

May vary inside declared ranges:

- Wardrobe set.
- Location within the same world.
- Emotional state.
- Lens/shot size.
- Platform framing and duration.
- Release-specific secondary motifs.

### Forbidden drift

Explicitly list visual clichés, voice behaviors, genres, gestures, colors,
locations, model artifacts and cultural references that would make the artist
generic or inconsistent.

## Reference set

Before native video generation, approve:

- Neutral front, profile and three-quarter face.
- Full body and proportions.
- Wardrobe front/back and material detail.
- Hair, makeup, hands and signature object.
- Voice/performance reference with consent and provenance.
- Emotion/gaze range.
- Short motion tests for walking, turning, singing and direct-camera delivery.
- Environment geography and recurring-object references.

Use clean, high-quality, internally consistent sources. Preserve reference IDs,
provider, owner, consent/license and approved use.

## Story engine

The artist world must sustain multiple releases. Define:

- Wound or unresolved pressure.
- Desire.
- Contradiction.
- Recurring obstacle.
- Transformation pattern.
- Recurring setting or social situation.
- Symbolic object.
- Repeatable audience question.

Each release uses:

`trigger → pursuit → escalation → irreversible image → emotional answer → loop`

Lyrics are evidence for the premise, not instructions to illustrate every line.

## Continuity receipt per shot

- Cast and identity reference IDs.
- Action before/during/after.
- Emotional state.
- Wardrobe, hair, makeup and props.
- Eyeline, screen direction and body position.
- Camera, lens, movement and duration.
- Lighting, location and story time.
- Spoken/sung line and lip-sync source.
- Provider, model/version, prompt, seed/settings and output ID.
- Selected take, defects, score and continuity verdict.

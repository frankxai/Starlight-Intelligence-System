## 1. Executive decisions

1. **Starlight Records is the umbrella rights, catalog, A&R, release, and revenue operating house.** It is the label system, not the public identity of every creative work.
2. **Arcanea Records is the canon-bound music imprint.** It releases Guardian music, world scores, virtual artists, and soundtracks that belong inside Arcanea.
3. **The primary human artist-name gate is reopened.** The leading recommendation is `FRANK OMEGA` for canonical DSP metadata and spoken billing, with `FRANK-Ω` as the visual sigil and `Frank Riemer` in legal, songwriter, and producer credits. This remains provisional pending approval and clearance.
4. **Plain FrankX remains the founder/masterbrand but is not recommended as the DSP artist name.** It has excellent stage energy and existing equity, but an active exact-name Italian house DJ/producer already releases in the same category and runs Tounka Records; shared-name mapping and search friction would be permanent operating debt.
5. **Alera is the first Arcanea virtual artist.** The remaining Guardians stay inside canon and content until Alera proves repeatable release demand.
6. **“Frank’s Vibes” stops behaving like a label.** It becomes **Vibe OS Sessions**, a playlist, content franchise, and functional-music product line under Frank Riemer and FrankX.
7. **Arcanea Cinema is a release line inside Arcanea Studios, not a new standalone company or social graph.** Anime Legends is the anime-native studio and production OS that can originate its own properties and co-produce Arcanea titles.
8. **Arcanea Editions is a product line, not another brand.** It turns selected images, stories, music, and films into digital editions, art books, prints, physical collector artifacts, and licensing packs.
9. **Streaming is discovery and cultural proof.** Owned audience, collector editions, licensing, education, community, and selective studio work are the economic engine.
10. **Do not mass-mint the image archive.** Thousands of generated images become a governed source archive. Curated collections become products; individual files do not become SKUs by default.
11. **NFTs are optional settlement and provenance rails, never the proposition.** Launch scarce, desirable artifacts first. Add tokens only when they improve ownership, access, patronage, resale, or licensing.
12. **One canon, multiple projections.** GitHub governs durable identities and policies; Supabase governs operational asset, rights, and release records; R2/Drive govern binaries; Notion governs decisions and workflows; distribution platforms remain downstream.

`FRANK OMEGA` wins the current strategy judgment because it passes the stage test, retains Frank, already exists inside the FrankX universe, extends from human performance to the FRANK-Ω avatar and cinema, and had no indexed exact artist collision in the preliminary Spotify/Apple/Traxsource/Beatport web audit. ## 4. The living-IP flywheel

Every high-value creative release should generate a connected artifact graph:

1. **World thesis** — emotion, transformation, realm, character, and audience promise.
2. **Hero work** — single, EP, story, character, short film, or visual collection.
3. **World assets** — images, motifs, scenes, symbols, lore, stems, scripts, and motion studies.
4. **Distribution fragments** — covers, Canvas loops, Shorts, Reels, stills, quotes, carousels, and trailers.
5. **Owned edition** — lossless audio, art, lore, making-of material, stems, wallpapers, or creator assets.
6. **Collector artifact** — numbered print, art book, vinyl, signed score, physical object, patron credit, or limited license.
7. **Learning product** — breakdown, template, prompt architecture, production case study, or academy module.
8. **Participation loop** — remix, fan art, world contribution, challenge, live session, or co-creation.
9. **Next canon event** — audience response and new work feed the next season.

The unit of production is therefore not “a song” or “an image.” It is a **world release package**.

## 5. Image archive to Arcanea Editions

### The rule

The archive is raw material. Taste is the business. No public gallery should expose thousands of undifferentiated outputs.

### Asset states

| State | Meaning | Public use |
|---|---|---|
| `ingested` | File captured with source metadata | None |
| `rights_unknown` | Provider terms, source, contributor, or consent unresolved | None |
| `working` | Eligible for iteration; not canon | Private only |
| `canon_candidate` | Passes taste and world-coherence review | Internal previews |
| `canon_locked` | Character/world identity approved | Production allowed |
| `release_approved` | Rights, quality, dimensions, and context cleared | Campaign use |
| `edition_master` | Color, crop, resolution, metadata, and print proof complete | Sale/licensing |
| `archived` | Superseded or rejected with lineage retained | None |

### Minimum provenance record

Every asset promoted beyond `working` requires:

- stable asset ID and content hash;
- creator/operator and contributors;
- source tool, model, version, account tier, and creation timestamp;
- source prompt and references, retained privately where sensitive;
- source-file location and derivative lineage;
- rights basis and allowed uses;
- depicted-person consent and trademark/copyright collision state;
- canon entity IDs, collection ID, release ID, and edition ID;
- human transformation notes, review decision, and reviewer;
- public disclosure posture.

### Collection architecture

Curate by world and emotional thesis, not by tool or prompt batch. A strong first collection structure is:

**ARCANEA: FIRST LIGHT**

- 12 hero artworks;
- one short story or lore chapter;
- one 4–6 track EP or visual album;
- one 3–8 minute cinematic piece;
- one digital art book;
- 3–5 physical print masters;
- one collector edition;
- one making-of creator case study.

Hundreds of archive images may contribute, but only the strongest 12 enter the visible collection. Scarcity comes from selection, context, finishing, and continuity—not an arbitrary token count.

### NFT posture

Do not launch “an NFT collection.” Launch a collector edition whose chain component is optional and legible.

Use tokenization only when it adds one of these:

- verifiable edition provenance;
- transferable access or patron status;
- transparent royalty or resale participation where legally reviewed;
- a machine-readable license;
- participation in a canon vote that does not compromise authorial integrity;
- cross-world identity through a future Starlight Passport.

The default first release is off-chain: numbered edition record, signed certificate, public provenance page, and private ownership ledger. Tokenize later without changing the underlying promise.

Use DistroKid as the near-term distributor, with enough artist slots for only approved identities. DistroKid assigns one slot to each unique primary artist and permits unlimited releases under that artist; collaborations can consume additional slots. Treat slots as scarce governance objects, not cheap invitations to spawn aliases. See the official [artist-slot guidance](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013534934-Using-Artist-Slots-to-Upload-Different-Releases-Under-Different-Artist-Names).

Release dates should be set with a minimum operational lead of 21 days. Spotify recommends pitching the focus track at least two weeks before release in its current [release guide](https://artists.spotify.com/blog/release-guide-preparing-for-release-day). The internal standard remains stricter so profile setup, editorial pitch, pre-save, content, rights, and QA have margin.

### Content grammar

Each hero release produces five recurring content forms:

- **The Signal** — the finished fragment: hook, scene, chorus, visual.
- **The Story** — why the work exists and what world it opens.

The feed should feel like an artist and studio, not an AI tool demo account. Tools appear in Build content; they never become the emotional proposition.

## 9. Physical releases and crowdfunding

### Sequence

1. Digital release and audience capture.
2. Paid digital collector edition.
3. Print-on-demand art print or book proof.
4. Paid preorder/deposit test.
5. Crowdfunded or preorder-funded physical run.
6. Inventory only for proven evergreen demand.

### Vinyl gate

Do not finance speculative vinyl inventory. Open vinyl only when one of these is true:

- 100 paid preorders;
- 300 qualified waitlist members with at least 30% deposit conversion;
- a partner covers manufacturing against committed distribution;
- a campaign is already fully funded.

Diggers Factory currently advertises vinyl runs from 100 units and a no-upfront preorder route; verify live economics and service terms before campaign launch on its [pressing page](https://www.diggersfactory.com/pressing). Use Bandcamp or the owned store for digital/direct fan relationships; use a specialist pressing partner for the physical object.

### Ideal physical product

The hero product is not “a vinyl record.” It is a **world artifact**:

- gatefold or art-book format;
- album or EP;
- selected Arcanea art;
- story/lore chapter;
- numbered certificate and provenance page;
- optional signed print or score;
- NFC/QR access to the living digital edition;
- patron names where consented.

Kickstarter or an owned preorder campaign becomes valuable when the object is ambitious enough to deserve collective creation. Crowdfunding is a story and demand-validation mechanism, not emergency financing.

## 10. Release operating system

### Release package contract

No track reaches distribution without:

- release ID, artist ID, imprint ID, collection ID, and campaign ID;
- lossless master, delivery master, instrumental, clean version where needed, and stems where strategically useful;
- ISRC/UPC ownership plan and metadata lock;
- credits, contributors, splits, consent, source terms, and disclosure state;
- square cover master and platform-safe derivatives;
- 9:16 loop, 1:1 loop, 16:9 visualizer or film, thumbnails, and stills;
- lyrics, translations, captions, alt text, and EPK copy;
- release page, smart link, pre-save, email, and collector offer;
- editorial pitch, sync tags, mood/tempo/key, and searchable descriptors;
- post-release measurement window and learning record.

### A&R gate

A release passes only when it satisfies four independent gates:

1. **Taste:** emotionally undeniable, no “good for AI” standard.
2. **Identity:** unmistakably belongs to this artist and season.
3. **Rights:** defensible provenance and contributor state.
4. **Leverage:** creates reusable audience, canon, product, or licensing value.

Volume never overrides a failed gate.

### Cadence

- One hero release every 3–4 weeks during the first season.
- One strong short-form fragment per hero work can recur in multiple edits; do not generate daily filler.
- One direct collector edition per 2–3 hero releases.
- One coherent 12-week season before introducing another artist.

## 13. Scorecard and kill rules

### North-star metrics

- qualified owned-audience growth;
- release-page to email/pass conversion;
- listener to buyer conversion;
- collector revenue per release;
- saves, repeat listeners, completion, and direct traffic—not raw plays alone;
- licensing conversations and placements;
- percentage of assets with complete provenance;
- time from green-light to complete release package;
- cross-world conversion without audience confusion.

### First-season proof targets

- three complete world release packages;
- 300 qualified owned contacts;
- 30–50 paying collectors across tiers;
- one production-ready physical preorder test;
- one sync-ready dossier and ten high-fit pitches;
- one Arcanea virtual-artist proof;
- zero rights-unknown assets published or sold.

### Kill rules

- Pause a new artist when the existing artist has not sustained a coherent 12-week season.
- Stop a channel after 12 high-quality posts when it produces no differentiated reach or conversion.
- Do not press physical inventory without paid demand.
- Do not mint when the token is the only reason the edition appears scarce.
- Archive any visual identity that cannot remain consistent across 20 outputs and motion.
- Reduce release volume before reducing taste, rights discipline, or narrative coherence.

### Boundary correction

The older Music IS doctrine named Excel/CSV as the entire catalog source of truth. The new boundary is narrower and stronger:

- Git-tracked CSV or release manifests are portable snapshots and batch interfaces.
- Supabase is the operational creative registry for high-volume asset and release records.
- GitHub remains the authority for identity, schemas, policies, and reviewed manifests.
- Notion is a writable workflow surface only where changes are promoted back through governed registry operations; it is not silent canon.

### Canon drift requiring resolution

1. Older GitHub declares Arcanea Records the umbrella; newer Notion declares Starlight Records the meta-label. This document chooses Starlight Records as umbrella and Arcanea Records as imprint.
2. Older GitHub models Frank Riemer, Frank’s Vibes, Arcanea, and Nona as four labels. This document separates artist, series, imprint, and future act.
3. `frankxai/arcanea-records` currently presents as a duplicate/general Arcanea repository rather than a dedicated label repository. Do not treat its name as proof of authority. Reconcile or archive/rename through a separate repository-governance decision.
4. The Empire Registry foundation remains a draft PR. Until merged, this strategy is a proposal and every human projection must retain its source branch/commit.

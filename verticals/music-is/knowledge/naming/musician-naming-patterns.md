# Musician Naming Patterns — Reference Library

> Distilled patterns from 60+ years of professional musician naming. Reference doc for `naming-intelligence` skill.

**Last updated:** 2026-04-29

---

## Why naming matters

A persona name caps the audience ceiling permanently. Bad-name-but-great-music is a tax that compounds against you for the artist's entire career. Good-name-but-OK-music is leverage that compounds in your favor.

Specific costs of a bad name:
- **Search cost** — fans can't find you; algorithms can't recommend you cleanly
- **Memorability cost** — fans who hear you once never recall you
- **Brand-extension cost** — you can't ship merch / collabs / sub-projects under it
- **Pronunciation cost** — fans who try to talk about you split into pronunciation factions, fragmenting word-of-mouth
- **Trademark cost** — eventually you collide with another act and must rename, losing audience equity

Specific value of a good name:
- **Compounds for decades** — Madonna, Bjork, Aphex Twin, Burial all benefit from name-quality 30+ years later
- **Reduces marketing spend** — name does brand-work the artist would otherwise pay for
- **Enables algorithmic discovery** — unique-enough names get clean playlist data
- **Survives genre pivots** — "Aphex Twin" or "Bjork" can do anything genre-wise; "DJ Trancemaster" cannot

## Pattern family 1 — Single-evocative

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Madonna | Pop | Iconic word; archetype-evocation |
| Bjork | Experimental | Unique single-word; phoneme-distinct |
| Burial | Electronic | Single-syllable; mood-direct; dark |
| Aphex Twin | Electronic | Custom phoneme cluster; sounds like the music |
| Sade | Soul | Distinct phoneme; one-of-a-kind |
| Beyonce | Pop | Custom-coined; rhythm-singable |
| Grimes | Indie/electronic | Single-word evocative; sonic |
| FKA twigs | Avant-pop | Lowercase styling = signal; unique |
| Lorde | Pop | One-syllable archetype |
| Halsey | Pop | Single-name evocative |
| Mitski | Indie | Single-name unique |

### Use when
- Persona is THE persona (single-artist label or hero-persona)
- Audience-ceiling needs to be very high
- Persona will pivot genres over career
- Memorability + searchability are top priorities

### Pitfalls
- Too-common single-word words conflict with existing artists (e.g., "Aurora" already taken; "Alexa" taken-and-Amazon-collisioned)
- Real-word single-names without distinguishing phonemes get lost
- "Branded" or "manufactured" feel if overworked (e.g., "Zaria" or "Kayza" can feel synthetic)

## Pattern family 2 — Mythic / archetype

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Aurora | Pop | Goddess archetype; sonic + mythic |
| Phoenix | Indie/pop | Iconic archetype; rebirth-mythos |
| Apollo (varies) | Multiple | Greek archetype |
| Daft Punk | Electronic | Subverted archetype (anti-name) |
| Cocteau Twins | Dream pop | Literary archetype |
| Massive Attack | Trip-hop | Threat-archetype |
| Sufjan Stevens | Folk | Anglo-Saxon historical archetype-feel |

### Use when
- Persona occupies an explicit archetypal space
- Mythic / fantasy / cinematic genre alignment
- Per-Guardian pattern (Music IS Arcanea label is canonical fit)

### Pitfalls
- Generic mythology pulls (e.g., "Zeus" or "Odin") feel stock
- Overused archetypes (e.g., "Aurora") have decreased searchability
- Misappropriation risk (e.g., using deities from cultures the artist isn't part of)

## Pattern family 3 — First-name-only or first-last-name

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Adele | Pop | First-name owns the SERP; iconic |
| Beyonce | Pop | First-name owns the brand |
| Olafur Arnalds | Neo-classical | First-last; Icelandic phoneme distinction |
| Max Richter | Post-classical | First-last; classical convention |
| Nils Frahm | Post-classical | First-last; Germanic |
| Hania Rani | Post-classical | First-last; modern post-classical |
| Joep Beving | Post-classical | First-last; understated |
| Jon Hopkins | Electronic + post-classical | First-last |
| Frank Ocean | R&B/avant | First-last evocative |
| Bon Iver | Indie folk | First-last evocative; quasi-French |

### Use when
- Persona IS the artist (the artist's actual name OR a stage-name in the same form)
- Genre convention favors first-last (classical, neo-classical, jazz, folk, singer-songwriter)
- The artist wants their name to be the brand

### Pitfalls
- Common names (e.g., "John Smith") have hopeless searchability
- Foreign-name pronunciation cost in target markets
- Doesn't extend to multi-persona (artist's name IS the persona)

## Pattern family 4 — Two-word evocative

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Bon Iver | Indie folk | Quasi-French; rhythmic |
| Daft Punk | Electronic | Punctuation of genre + attitude |
| Massive Attack | Trip-hop | Threat-archetype |
| Cocteau Twins | Dream pop | Literary-evocative |
| Iron Maiden | Metal | Imagery-direct |
| Pearl Jam | Rock | Custom-evocative |
| Vampire Weekend | Indie | Unexpected pairing |
| Gorillaz | Alt | Single-word but evocative-by-misspell |
| Tame Impala | Psych | Animal-mythic-contradiction |

### Use when
- One word doesn't carry enough specificity
- Genre/mood needs a second-word punctuator
- Persona is a band/group OR persona-multiplied-under-name

### Pitfalls
- Two generic words concatenated feel forgettable
- Too-precious or too-clever pairings age poorly
- Trademark conflict risk doubles (need to clear both words + the combo)

## Pattern family 5 — Compound / coined

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Soundgarden | Grunge | Compound-evocative; nature-mythic |
| Radiohead | Alt-rock | Compound-evocative; anti-broadcast |
| Coldplay | Alt-rock | Compound-coined |
| Soundtrack of Our Lives | Indie | Compound-phrase |
| Mogwai | Post-rock | Coined word (cinema-reference) |
| Sigur Ros | Post-rock | Coined-Icelandic |

### Use when
- A custom-coined word can carry rhythm + meaning + canon
- Persona occupies novel-sound space and wants a novel-name

### Pitfalls
- Overcooked/forced coinages feel artificial
- Pronunciation chaos if coining is too clever
- Trademark process can be harder for novel words (if it sounds-too-much-like existing brand)

## Pattern family 6 — Codename / acronym

### Examples

| Name | Genre | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| SBTRKT | Electronic | Vowel-removed coinage |
| RJD2 | Hip-hop/electronic | Initials + number |
| MGMT | Indie/electronic | Acronym-evocative |
| ODESZA | Electronic | Capitalized custom-word |
| Daft Punk | Electronic | Already-evocative |
| MF DOOM | Hip-hop | Acronym + persona-name |
| LCD Soundsystem | Indie/dance | Acronym + descriptor |

### Use when
- A private-meaning encoding matters
- Anti-glamour / anti-influencer aesthetic
- Genre supports it (electronic, hip-hop, indie)

### Pitfalls
- Most acronyms aren't memorable; works only when phonetic AND distinctive
- Search-engine treatment of codenames is sometimes hostile (especially numbers in name)
- Not aspirational — feels manufactured-or-tech-y

## Genre-pattern affinity map (recommended pattern per genre)

| Genre | Recommended pattern primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Neo-classical / cinematic-piano | Pattern 3 (first-last) | Pattern 1 (single-evocative) |
| Lo-fi / chill-house / electronic-vibe | Pattern 1 (single-evocative) OR Pattern 5 (compound) | Pattern 4 (two-word) |
| Cinematic / mythic / score-grade | Pattern 2 (mythic-archetype) | Pattern 1 (single-evocative) |
| Punk / alt / cathartic | Pattern 5 (compound-brutalist) OR Pattern 6 (codename) | Pattern 1 (single-raw) |
| Pop | Pattern 1 (single-evocative-iconic) | Pattern 3 (first-name-only) |
| Hip-hop | Pattern 6 (codename) | Pattern 1 (single-evocative) |
| Indie | Pattern 4 (two-word) | Pattern 5 (compound) |
| Electronic | Pattern 1, 5, OR 6 | varies |

## What to avoid (cross-pattern)

- **Names of any specific identifiable existing artist** (refuses copyright-flirting)
- **Slurs, ableist terms, or terms with active reclamation contention**
- **Generic genre words alone** ("DJ", "MC", "Producer")
- **Numerical-only names** (forgettable, unsearchable)
- **Misappropriation** (deities, ethnic-specific terms, sacred names from cultures the artist isn't part of)
- **Names that mimic non-music brand trademarks**
- **Names already used by an existing Music IS persona**
- **Three-or-more-word names** (forgettability cliff after 2 words)

# Release, rights and software operations

## Cadence: generation and publication have different jobs

First 90-day pilot: three strong Frank X singles, roughly 3–4 weeks apart after the first packet clears. Keep two backups. A distinct piano catalog may publish every 4–6 weeks if admitted; stagger campaigns so the label has one flagship attention window. One virtual/collective pilot at a time. Increase flagship cadence to two weeks only with a six-week finished backlog, reliable delivery and audience/cost evidence.

Weekly private creation and auditions can continue inside an approved budget. There is no per-tool-output release quota. Spotify allows one pending editorial pitch; its current guidance requires at least seven days for relevant release/pitch handling. Use **28 days internal delivery lead** and submit the pitch as soon as eligible. Neither pitching nor release frequency guarantees placement. Recheck provider policy during each packet.

## Weekly owner rhythm

Monday: 45-minute listening/rights council; choose at most three commitments and record the strongest reason against the lead track. Midweek: finish the selected recording and campaign family. Friday: review live/profile evidence and listener feedback where a release actually exists. Monthly: statements, expenses, rights exceptions and retained-master restore. Review 7/28-day campaign outcomes using actual provider receipts.

Track voluntary repeat listeners, saves/listener with denominator, release-page-to-listening clicks, permissioned First Light signups, substantive replies and attributable receipts/costs. Artist listener counts and views alone cannot choose the next persona. Historical revenue and popularity claims need actual evidence.

## Release packet and state axes

Keep creative, rights, canon and delivery status independent. A&R approval does not clear rights; a submission receipt does not prove correct live placement.

| Stage | Evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Original audio, source URL/export ID, hashes, exact lyrics/prompt, model/settings, creation and download dates | Catalog operator identifies duplicates and unknowns |
| A&R | Listening files, bracket, voice/album fit, exact selected master revision | Frank chooses |
| Rights | Component authorship, licenses, consent, actual splits and term/territory | Rights owner and qualified reviewer resolve scope |
| Finishing | Technical analysis plus human listening; artwork/captions/credits | Named producer and Frank review |
| Packet | Work/recording/release IDs, artist IDs, exact audio/artwork/packet hashes, budget and channel scope | Authorized owner signs exact revision |
| Submission | Supported/manual platform action and actual receipt | Operator records submitted or uncertain |
| Live | Correct artist mapping and checked public URLs | Operator records live verified |
| Accounting | Statements mapped to identifiers and signed agreements; unmatched lines in suspense | Entity approves allocation and payments |

Retain a recording's identifier across the same master re-release; assign new recording/version identities only when justified under identifier guidance. Distinguish work from recording. Do not mint guessed ISRCs or mark a platform action complete from a local status toggle.

Changing the master, artwork, metadata or packet invalidates old authorization. Freeze the approved packet. Timeout after possible provider acceptance means **uncertain**: reconcile before retrying. Adapter idempotency uses tenant + release ID + packet revision + target + operation, with a durable effect ledger.

No single software package makes a label legally compliant. A rights ledger, appropriate agreements, accounting and truthful platform delivery are the functions that matter. Specialist royalty software becomes useful when external artists/statements create an actual administration bottleneck.

## Dutch/EU release checklist

1. Verify the real contracting entity, current KVK record and actual activities. Add Arcanea Records as a trade name where appropriate, or form an entity only for a justified business reason. No BV was verified or formed here.
2. Complete exact and similar name review in KVK/BOIP and EUIPO/TMview for actual territories and goods/services. Trademark and trade-name registration are separate. Counsel should choose the filing scope; music commonly points toward recorded music and entertainment, but merchandise/software are separate planned uses.
3. Distinguish human composition/lyrics, master control, performance, samples, artwork and synthetic voice permissions. Record actual contributor splits in each rights pool, not the old automatic 45/25/20/10 cascade.
4. Suno's guidance edited 3 September 2026 grants commercial use to songs downloaded while paid and explicitly does not guarantee copyright. Preserve creation **and** download date, relevant paid-plan evidence, source versions and applicable terms. Review historical catalog permissions individually rather than retroactively applying one new FAQ.
5. BumaStemra registration distinguishes human lyrics/composition from generated components; wholly generated works without qualifying human creative contribution cannot be registered there. Assess actual contributions. Sena concerns neighboring rights and actual musician/producer roles; do not infer eligibility from a composition registration.
6. DistroKid accepts AI music conditionally on rights, no impersonation/infringement and no mass-generated spam. Resolve exact releases against current terms; platform acceptance is not a legal opinion.
7. Keep accounting records and invoices under applicable retention rules; official Dutch guidance generally specifies at least seven years. Maintain private contributor/customer data lawfully, document newsletter consent and suppression, and review applicable synthetic-media transparency duties for the actual release format with counsel.
8. Recheck whether particular generated recordings meet exclusive-rights requirements before Content ID or commercial sync promises. License only controlled rights.

For outside artists commission a finite, clear master license or service contract, component split sheet, session performer agreement and separate voice-model consent where used. Publishing, world IP, merchandise and adaptations need their own express terms. Do not silently acquire every artist's background IP.

## Spotify and YouTube

Claim/map the correct Spotify for Artists profile after the distributor provides genuine IDs. Shared artist names create discovery and mapping work; DistroKid documents correction paths but does not grant name rights. Artist slots are permanently used by primary-artist/collaboration uploads under current DistroKid guidance, so audit before spending a slot.

Use a Frank X artist YouTube channel and one Arcanea Records/world listening destination with roster playlists. Add a dedicated piano channel/OAC only when that separate catalog is admitted and eligibility is met. YouTube OAC requires a channel focused on a single artist/band and an official distributed release plus partner/program criteria; the label hub is not every artist's OAC. Do not rename existing channels blindly.

Spotify's August 2026 AI Persona policy begins rollout in mid-September and excludes such identities from editorial/algorithmic recommendations by default, with follower-related exceptions. It concerns public identity, distinct from AI contribution credits. Virtual acts must be transparent and build direct fandom; do not model their economics on default recommendations or hide synthetic membership. A real producer using generative tools remains truthfully human, with accurate generated-component credits.

Start with YouTube archive, one short-video discovery channel and First Light. One single yields two scene-led hook cuts, one process/performance asset and one launch film/visualizer where useful. Reuse the family; do not launch five social accounts per persona.

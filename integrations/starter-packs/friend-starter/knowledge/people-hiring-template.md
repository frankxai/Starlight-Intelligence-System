# People Hiring Template — Worked Example

> Ana-grade hiring system applied to a real role: **Senior People Operations Manager for a 50-person consultancy**. Anonymized but realistic. Demonstrates the full sub-system: ICP → interview architecture → calibration → fit assessment → debrief → onboarding. Sub-system 1 of 6 in the People Intelligence Domain Sub-Stack (renamed from HR Intelligence at v7.6.0).

**Disclaimer:** Hiring decisions touch employment law and protected-class considerations. This template is system architecture, not legal advice. Validate jurisdiction-specific compliance with qualified counsel.

---

## Scenario

- **Company:** "Northway Strategy" (anonymized) — 50-person management consultancy, US-based, NY HQ with remote staff in CA, IL, MA.
- **Stage:** Established (10 years old, profitable, no outside capital).
- **Role:** Senior People Operations Manager. First dedicated People Ops hire — currently distributed across the COO and an external HR consultant.
- **Outcome the role unlocks:** Build the People Ops function so the COO can focus on client delivery and the external consultant can be retired. Raise the floor on hiring, performance, and retention practices that are currently inconsistent.
- **Jurisdiction:** US. Multi-state (NY, CA, IL, MA).

---

## Step 1 — ICP

### Outcome the role unlocks

Translate Northway's ad-hoc people practices into a calibrated People Ops function — hiring, onboarding, performance, and retention systems — that scales from 50 to 100 without the COO continuing to absorb HR work.

### Team gap analysis

The team currently lacks:

- **Skill gap:** Internal HR expertise. Hiring runs on partner intuition; onboarding is an email and a 1:1; performance reviews are inconsistent across practices.
- **Perspective gap:** Practitioner who has built People Ops INSIDE a professional services firm — not a tech-company People Ops who will try to import OKRs and 360s wholesale.
- **Energy gap:** Structuring energy. Northway has strong generative energy (partners) and execution energy (consultants) but no one whose default move is "let me build the system that prevents this from being a one-off conversation."
- **Lived-experience gap:** Has scaled a People Ops function from 0-1 in a similar context (50 → 150-person professional services firm).

### Cognitive requirements

- Synthesis across stakeholder groups (partners, consultants, support staff) under conflicting priorities
- Translation of ambiguous partner asks into structured systems
- Quantitative reasoning for compensation banding, retention math, hiring funnel analysis
- Pattern recognition across qualitative input (employee feedback) and quantitative (engagement, retention)
- Strategic abstraction — moving from "fix this one performance review issue" to "redesign the review process"

### Behavioral signals

1. **Builds-systems-from-anecdotes:** Hears 3 partner complaints about hiring quality and produces a calibrated rubric, not 3 fixes.
2. **Stakeholder-translation:** Translates partner asks into employee-facing language and back, without losing fidelity to either.
3. **Structuring-under-ambiguity:** Walks into "we need a performance review process" with a discovery loop, not a template.
4. **Process-as-instrument:** Treats every process (hiring, onboarding, perf) as a measurable instrument, not a checklist.
5. **Tactical-credibility:** Will run the search themselves if the loop is broken; will not delegate everything to a recruiter.
6. **Reads-the-room:** Knows when to push back on a partner ask vs. when to absorb and run.
7. **Compliance-fluency:** Knows the difference between NY pay-transparency, CA salary-history bans, and EU GDPR — without needing to be reminded.

### Work-history patterns

- **Pattern A:** 8-12 years in People Ops, including at least one 0→1 People Ops build at a 50-150 person professional services firm.
- **Pattern B:** Mix of in-house People Ops + boutique HR consulting (gives the stakeholder-translation muscle).
- **Pattern C (acceptable):** Strong tech-company People Ops generalist who has explicitly chosen to leave tech for professional services and can articulate why (red-flag: "tech is too political"; green-flag: "I'm interested in the partnership economics and the longer feedback loops").

### Culture-add hypothesis

Northway is run by partners who think of People Ops as overhead. This candidate adds: a People Ops practitioner who frames the function as **leverage on partner time and client quality**, not as administrative cost. Bridges the perspective gap (HR-as-leverage, not HR-as-cost) and the energy gap (structuring orientation in a generative-and-execution-heavy team). Productive tension: will push partners to formalize practices that have been informal for 10 years; tension is stage-appropriate at 50 employees.

### DOES-NOT-MATTER list

- [ ] HR certification (SHRM-CP, SHRM-SCP, etc.) — does not predict performance in this role
- [ ] MBA — irrelevant; depth in People Ops > generalist business credentials
- [ ] School prestige — uncorrelated with People Ops practitioner quality
- [ ] Years-of-experience floor above 8 — strong 8-year practitioner > weak 15-year practitioner
- [ ] "Executive presence" — this is a builder role, not a board-room role
- [ ] Big-4 or Big-Tech pedigree — may even cut against us; we want professional services context
- [ ] Always-on availability — bias signal, illegal proxy in many contexts

### Compliance flag — for legal counsel

- **Pay transparency:** NY requires salary range in JD ($-$); CA requires upon request; IL has reporting; MA has new rules effective 2025.
- **Salary history:** NY, CA, MA all ban asking salary history.
- **Right-to-work:** US I-9 process applies.
- **Candidate data retention:** Federal + state records retention rules apply.
- **Multi-state hiring:** Tax/employment registration in CA, IL, MA confirmed already (for existing remote employees) — confirm with counsel that hiring this role doesn't trigger new state registrations.

**Action: forwarded to legal counsel before publishing JD.**

## Step 2 — Interview architecture

### Loop overview (5 slots, ~5 hours total candidate time)

- **Slot 1 — Initial structured behavioral (45 min)** — COO (hiring manager)
- **Slot 2 — Work sample: People Ops case (90 min, take-home + 30-min walkthrough)** — VP Operations + 1 partner
- **Slot 3 — Domain-deep structured behavioral (60 min)** — External HR consultant (current state-of-People-Ops at Northway)
- **Slot 4 — Cross-functional structured behavioral (45 min)** — 2 senior consultants (employee perspective)
- **Slot 5 — Senior leadership structured behavioral (45 min)** — Managing Partner

**Decision-fatigue mitigations:**
- All slots scheduled before lunch or after 3pm
- No interviewer carries more than 2 slots in one day
- Slot 5 (Managing Partner) deliberately last — partner instinct should validate, not lead

### Slot 1 — Initial structured behavioral (COO)

**Dimensions assessed:** Stakeholder-translation, Structuring-under-ambiguity, Reads-the-room, Tactical-credibility, Builds-systems-from-anecdotes.

#### Question stems with rationale

**Dimension: Builds-systems-from-anecdotes**

- **Q1.1:** "Tell me about a time three different stakeholders complained about the same problem. What did you do?"
  - **Rationale:** Surfaces whether the candidate goes to system-level (calibrated rubric, process redesign) or stays at fix-the-one-thing level. Predicts whether they'll build the People Ops function or just react to incoming requests.
  - **Probe if shallow:** "What was the system you built? Walk me through it."
  - **Red-flag pattern:** "I fixed each one individually" without naming a system shift.

- **Q1.2:** "Tell me about a time the system you built didn't work and you had to revise it."
  - **Rationale:** Surfaces whether they treat their own work as an instrument (measurable, revisable) or as a final answer.
  - **Probe if shallow:** "What was the signal that made you revise?"
  - **Red-flag pattern:** Cannot name a specific signal; revision was vibes-driven.

#### Rubric — Dimension: Builds-systems-from-anecdotes

| Score | Behavioral anchor |
|-------|-------------------|
| **5 — Strong hire signal** | Took 3+ stakeholder complaints, identified root cause, designed a calibrated process or rubric, deployed it, measured impact, revised based on signal. Specific deliverable named. |
| **4** | 5-anchor minus the measurement/revision step. Designed the system; didn't close the measurement loop. |
| **3 — Bar** | Took 2-3 complaints, designed a structural fix (process or rubric), deployed it. Limited revision evidence. |
| **2** | Took complaints, designed a fix-each-individually solution. Rationalized the absence of a system. |
| **1 — Strong no-hire signal** | Did not move from anecdote to system. Treated each complaint as a one-off. Cannot articulate why systems would have helped. |

(Full rubric for all 5 dimensions per slot. Truncated here for template length. The full file builds out every dimension across all 5 slots.)

### Slot 2 — Work sample: People Ops case

**Prompt (delivered 5 days before walkthrough):**

> "Northway is a 50-person consultancy with no internal People Ops function. Hiring runs on partner intuition; onboarding is an email and a 1:1; performance reviews are inconsistent across practices. The COO has identified retention as the biggest risk in the next 18 months — we've lost 3 strong consultants in the past year, all to firms with stronger People Ops practices.
>
> In a 5-page memo (or equivalent slide deck), produce a 12-month plan for building the People Ops function. Address: hiring, onboarding, performance reviews, retention. Identify what you would build first, what you would build sequentially, what you would NOT build in year one and why. Include a single page on how you would measure whether the function is working.
>
> You will walk through this with the VP Operations and one partner for 30 minutes."

**Grading rubric:** 5 dimensions — strategic sequencing, measurement design, professional-services context awareness, prioritization rigor, executive communication. Behavioral anchors at 1, 3, 5 for each.

**Rationale:** This is the single highest-validity slot in the loop. Per Schmidt & Hunter, work samples are second only to cognitive ability for predictive validity, and for this role, work-sample > cognitive-ability because the role is structuring-and-translation-heavy, not raw-cognition-heavy.

(Full structure mirrors Slot 1: 5-7 dimensions per slot, question stems with rationale, behavioral anchors at 1/3/5. Each slot tests a different dimension cluster from the ICP. Truncated in this template.)

## Step 3 — Calibration session

**60-minute pre-loop session, 4 raters: COO, VP Operations, External HR consultant, Managing Partner.**

(Senior consultants in Slot 4 attend a separate 30-minute calibration micro-session focused only on their dimensions — they aren't decision-rights raters, but they must score consistently.)

### Anchor candidates selected

- **Anchor 1:** "The Director of People we hired in 2023 who built the EU office's HR practice from 0 — strong retrospective hire-yes."
- **Anchor 2:** "The People Ops Manager we hired in 2022 who left at month 9, citing 'culture mismatch' — retrospective hire-no, in retrospect a similarity-attraction-driven hire."

### Rubric walk-through findings

In the live walk-through, the team surfaced:

- **Dim 2 (Stakeholder-translation):** COO and Managing Partner had different paraphrases of "5." COO said "translates partner asks into employee language without softening." MP said "communicates clearly across levels." These are not the same. Agreed language: "translates between stakeholder groups without losing fidelity to either, including holding partner asks accountable to employee-impact reality."
- **Dim 5 (Tactical-credibility):** All 4 raters had subtly different "3" anchors. Agreed: "3 = will roll up sleeves on a stuck task to unblock; will not be precious about the work."

### Anchor candidate scoring

When raters scored the two anchor candidates against the new rubric:

- Anchor 1: Median 4.2 — confirmed retrospective hire-yes signal.
- Anchor 2: Median 3.4 — concerning retroactively. The 3.4 should have been a no-hire under the new decision rule (median ≥4 on ≥75% of dimensions). The team's retrospective: yes, this hire was made on similarity-attraction (Anchor 2 had a background closely matching the COO's), and the rubric would have caught it.

This is the highest-leverage finding of the entire calibration: the new system would have prevented a known miss-hire.

### Decision rule pre-committed

- Hire: median ≥4 on ≥75% of dimensions AND ≥3 on every dimension.
- Tie-breaks default to hire-no.
- Inter-rater dispersion ≥2 points on a load-bearing dimension triggers single-dimension re-interview.

## Step 4 — Fit assessment (for one finalist candidate, "Candidate M")

### Team-as-of-now snapshot

- **Skills:** Heavy on consulting, client delivery, partner-track sales. Light on internal People Ops, talent development.
- **Perspectives:** All 4 partners + COO are from large management consulting firms (McKinsey/Bain/BCG-track). Light on professional-services-People-Ops specifically.
- **Energy types:** Generative (partners) + execution (consultants). Almost no structuring energy.
- **Lived experience:** All have scaled CONSULTING practices; none has scaled a People Ops FUNCTION inside a consulting practice.

### Candidate-adds inventory (Candidate M)

- **Skill add:** Built the People Ops function at a 60-person legal-services firm from 0 to 1; explicitly the kind of professional-services-context experience the team lacks.
- **Perspective add:** Frames People Ops as leverage on partner time, not as administrative cost. Articulated this explicitly in Slot 5 with the Managing Partner: "Your partners' marginal hour is worth $X to the firm; if I save them 4 hours a week of HR work and raise hiring quality 20%, the math is unambiguous."
- **Energy add:** Strong structuring energy. In Slot 2 (work sample), produced a 12-month plan that sequenced hiring → onboarding → performance → retention, with measurement points throughout. This is structuring energy applied to a function that has had none.
- **Lived-experience add:** Has navigated partner pushback before. Articulated specifically how she earned partner trust at her previous firm: "I delivered a hiring loop redesign in 60 days that immediately raised quality. Partners trusted me on the bigger work because I had earned it on the smaller work."

### Gap-bridge analysis

| Team gap | Does Candidate M close it? | Notes |
|----------|----------------------------|-------|
| Skill gap (internal HR) | Yes — strongly | 8 years People Ops, professional-services context |
| Perspective gap (HR-as-leverage) | Yes — strongly | Articulated the leverage frame unprompted in Slot 5 |
| Energy gap (structuring) | Yes — strongly | Slot 2 work sample is structuring-energy in pure form |
| Lived-experience gap (0→1 in PS context) | Yes — strongly | Built it before, in a similar context |

**Net gap-bridge finding:** Closes 4 of 4 named gaps strongly. Meaningful culture-add.

### Tension analysis

- **Productive tension:** Will push partners to formalize practices that have been informal for 10 years. Stage-appropriate at 50 employees; partners need to be on board for this. Managing Partner explicitly endorsed in Slot 5.
- **Friction risk:** Strong opinions on hiring process. May clash with the partner who has historically run hiring on instinct. Hiring manager (COO) flagged: aligned, sees this as net positive.

### Bias self-check

- [x] No "adds" proxy for protected-class. Verified.
- [x] No "gaps" actually mean "not similar enough to the team." (In fact, Candidate M is differentiated from the partner profile — that is the culture-add.)
- [x] Assessment is not overriding rubric — rubric scores already strongly hire-yes (median 4.4 across dimensions). Fit is supplement, confirms.

### Net finding

Candidate M is a meaningful culture-add. Closes all 4 named team gaps strongly. Productive tension is real and stage-appropriate. Recommend proceed to debrief; rubric and fit converge on hire-yes.

## Step 5 — Debrief + Decision Record (Candidate M)

### Pre-debrief structured scores

| Dimension | COO | VP Ops | HR Consultant | MP | Median |
|-----------|-----|--------|---------------|-----|--------|
| Builds-systems-from-anecdotes | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4.5 |
| Stakeholder-translation | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4.5 |
| Structuring-under-ambiguity | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4.5 |
| Process-as-instrument | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4.0 |
| Tactical-credibility | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4.0 |
| Reads-the-room | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4.5 |
| Compliance-fluency | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4.0 |

**High-dispersion dimensions:** Tactical-credibility (MP scored 3, others 4-5; 2-point spread).

### Discussion of dispersion

**Tactical-credibility dimension:**

- MP anchored 3 to: "Candidate spoke about a time she rolled up sleeves on a hiring loop, but I wanted to see her articulate WHY she didn't delegate that to a recruiter."
- HR Consultant anchored 5 to: "Specifically because she didn't delegate; she built credibility by doing the work, then earned the right to redesign."
- COO and VP Ops anchored 4 to: same observation, slight uncertainty on whether it scales.

**Bias pattern named by facilitator:** Possible halo on MP's other dimensions (5s on Stakeholder-translation and Reads-the-room) — the strong leadership-presence read may be inflating those, while a real rigor on Tactical-credibility surfaces a 3.

**Resolution:** MP, after re-anchoring to the specific behavioral evidence, updated to a 4. New median: 4.25.

### Final decision

- Median ≥4 on ≥75% of dimensions: YES (7 of 7 dimensions ≥4).
- Min ≥3 on every dimension: YES.
- **Decision: HIRE.**

**Decision rationale (rubric-anchored, retained):**

> Hire. Median 4.4 across raters and dimensions; ≥4 on every load-bearing dimension; min 4.0. Fit assessment confirms culture-add via 4-of-4 named gap closures. Halo flagged on Tactical-credibility; surfaced and re-anchored without changing the net-hire signal. No protected-class references in any rater rationale. Productive tension expected on process-formalization in months 2-4; Managing Partner aligned. 90-day onboarding architecture below.

### Learning capture

**What worked:**
- Calibration session caught the Stakeholder-translation paraphrase mismatch; would have been a 2-point dispersion in debrief otherwise.
- Anchor candidate scoring revealed the 2022 miss-hire as a similarity-attraction-driven decision; built collective trust in the new system.
- Work sample (Slot 2) produced clearest signal across all dimensions; carry forward — this is the highest-validity slot for People Ops roles.

**What drifted:**
- MP's halo effect on Slot 5; flagged in debrief but should be primed in next calibration session ("MP, you'll see strong leadership presence; calibrate for whether each dimension is independently anchored").

**Feed forward to next People Ops calibration session:**
- Sharpen Tactical-credibility anchor language (MP's 3 was driven by absent rationale; anchor should specify "candidate articulates WHY they did the work themselves vs. delegating").

## Step 6 — 90-day onboarding architecture (Candidate M, hire-yes)

### Day 1-7: Land

- **Day 1:** Manager (COO) 1:1 (60 min) — confirm role, expectations, week-1 milestone (read all existing People Ops artifacts + meet COO + 2 partners).
- **Day 2-3:** Structured intros — partners, practice leads, senior consultants. 30-min slots, agenda provided in advance.
- **Day 4-5:** First small ownership — produce the new-hire onboarding checklist for the next two consultants joining in 3 weeks. Real deliverable, scoped small.
- **Day 7:** Manager 1:1 (30 min) — calibrate week 1; surface any early friction.

### Day 8-30: Learn

- Weekly Manager 1:1 (45 min).
- 30-day milestone: Hiring loop redesign for the open Senior Consultant role — calibrated rubric, structured slots, calibration session before the loop. (Ana's full hiring sub-system, applied.)
- Day 30: Manager + Managing Partner + 2 senior-consultant peer feedback (structured, brief).

### Day 31-60: Contribute

- Bi-weekly Manager 1:1.
- 60-day milestone: Performance review process v1 — proposal, partner alignment, deployment for one practice as pilot.
- Day 60: Manager 1:1 + practice-lead feedback.

### Day 61-90: Own

- Bi-weekly Manager 1:1.
- 90-day milestone: 12-month People Ops roadmap delivered to Managing Partner — sequencing, milestones, measurement plan. (Year-1 build of the function.)
- Day 90: Manager + Managing Partner + cross-functional feedback. Decision: confirmed.

### Manager-side rituals

- Calibrate expectations weekly for 30 days, then bi-weekly.
- Front-load energizing work (structuring, design) — Candidate M's Talent profile shows this is energy-positive.
- Surface friction at week 4-6 (when partner pushback on process formalization will likely emerge), not at month 3.
- Managing Partner 1:1 at day 45 specifically — Candidate M's success is partly a function of MP cover when partners push back.

## What this template demonstrates

- **The full sub-system end-to-end** — ICP → architecture → calibration → fit → debrief → onboarding, on a single role.
- **Refusal of HR fluff** — no "great culture fit," no "team player," no "executive presence." Every signal is behavioral and rubric-anchored.
- **Research grounding** — Schmidt & Hunter validity bundle (cognitive/work-sample + structured behavioral); Project Oxygen calibration; decision-fatigue scheduling; culture-add over culture-fit; 90-day onboarding as retention predictor.
- **Bias-correction operationalized** — named patterns flagged in real time by the facilitator; structured scores before discussion; tie-breaks default to hire-no.
- **Compliance discipline** — disclaimer present; protected-class proxy check explicit; all question stems flagged for counsel review.
- **Voice composition** — interviewer briefs and stakeholder-facing materials run in Ana's voice via Genius Profile (referenced in this template; live in the actual instance).

This is the instrument. Most hiring rooms run on first-impression bias dressed in structured-sounding conversation; the instrument prevents what the conversation cannot.

# People Performance Intelligence — Template + Filled Examples

> Format Starlight fills in when producing Performance Intelligence artifacts (People Intelligence sub-stack — renamed from HR Intelligence at v7.6.0). Five sections — review redesign, coaching session, feedback rehearsal, difficult-conversation rehearsal, mediation session — each with empty template + Ana-grade filled example. Realistic anonymized. All five carry heavy disclaimers because performance work touches legal documentation, clinical boundaries, and protected concerns.

---

## Disclaimer (non-waivable, applies to all five sections)

**This is rehearsal architecture and organizational design thinking grounded in research (SBI from Center for Creative Leadership; SCARF from David Rock / NeuroLeadership Institute; three-conversations from Stone, Patton, Heen / Harvard Negotiation Project; solution-focused from de Shazer and Berg; threat-response neuroscience from Lieberman and Eisenberger; annual-rating dismantling case studies from Deloitte, Adobe, Microsoft, GE).**

**It is NOT:**
1. **Legal counsel** — termination/demotion/discipline documentation requires employment counsel review BEFORE delivery
2. **Psychotherapy** — clinical-level distress requires clinical referral, not coaching
3. **Investigation** — harassment, discrimination, retaliation, whistleblower content require legal + investigation, not mediation

**Built-in refusals:**
- PIPs functioning as termination scaffolding (refuse and route to honest termination)
- Stack rank and forced curves (research-backed harm; refuse to design)
- Annual-rating-as-growth-tool (refuse; redesign)
- Mediating harassment, discrimination, retaliation, substantial-power-imbalance conflicts (route to legal + investigation)
- Coaching as therapy (route to clinical)
- "You are X" personality-attacking feedback (refuse at SBI quality gate)

```markdown
# Performance Review Redesign Plan — Marcus & Lane Architects — 2026-04-24

> **This is rehearsal architecture and organizational design thinking, not legal counsel and not HRIS implementation. Run the redesign past employment counsel in your jurisdiction before rollout.**

## Context
- **Organization:** Marcus & Lane Architects (anonymized)
- **Size:** mid (78 employees, 4 offices in NL/DE/UK)
- **Current ritual:** annual rating with 9-box stack-rank calibration grid
- **Start quarter:** 2026-Q3
- **Regulatory context:** NL works council notification required; UK and DE offices have local employment counsel coordination
- **Hiring calibration grammar referenced:** yes — `people-intelligence/hiring/calibration-marcus-lane-2026-q1.md` (calibration vocabulary transfers from hiring debriefs)

## Diagnosis — what's broken

The current 9-box ritual was inherited from a 2019 consultancy engagement and has produced three patterns Marcus & Lane leadership has noticed: top-rated employees ("future leaders") leave at higher rates than middle-rated employees (research-consistent — high performers detect that the rating system uses them to justify the bottom box more than to develop them); middle-rated employees disengage in the calibration window (Q4) because the rating system feels like a moving target; and the 9-box conversation has become decoupled from comp — partners report that comp decisions get made in a separate room with mostly different inputs, which means the 9-box is doing the demoralizing work without doing the comp work it claims to do. This matches the practitioner-readable research from Adobe, Microsoft, GE on annual-rating dismantling. Marcus & Lane is not unusual; it has just noticed.

## Three-layer architecture

### Layer 1 — Continuous SBI feedback
- **Cadence:** bi-weekly 1:1, 30 min, manager + report
- **Format:** low-friction — no form, conversation IS the artifact; manager keeps own notes for memory
- **Tooling refusal:** declined the proposal to add Lattice ratings/badges; gamified continuous feedback re-imports the threat response we are dismantling
- **Manager script — opener (template):**
  > "Before we get into the work, anything you want to bring? And one thing I want to share: <SBI sentence>."

### Layer 2 — Quarterly growth conversation (NO rating)
- **Cadence:** quarterly, 75 min, blocked off ≥4 weeks before/after Layer 3 comp window
- **Structure:** what's working / what's stuck / what's next
- **What is NOT in this conversation:** rating language, comp language, promotion signal, dollars
- **Manager script — opener:**
  > "This is our quarterly growth conversation. It's not your performance review in the old sense — there's no rating today, and comp/promotion get decided in a separate process in October. Today is for: where are you flowing, where are you stuck, and what does next quarter look like."
- **Manager script — three-part prompts:**
  > "Working: where have you felt momentum or growth in the last 90 days?"
  > "Stuck: where has the work felt heavier than it should, or where has growth stalled?"
  > "Next: what's the focus you want to invest in for next quarter?"
- **Employee-prep guide (≤30 min prep, sent 7 days before):**
  > "Bring three things: one piece of work you're proud of from this quarter; one stuck point or block you've been sitting with; one development area you want focus on next quarter. You don't need to prepare a self-rating, defend your performance, or justify your existence here — that's not what this conversation is for."

### Layer 3 — Annual compensation and promotion review (calibrated, separated)
- **Cadence:** annual, October calibration window; comp decisions communicated to employees in second week of November
- **Calibration meeting structure:** 2-hour partner+director session; calibration in cross-office pairs (NL+UK, NL+DE) to avoid friendly-pair inflation
- **Decision-rights map:** comp decision rests with the office MD + the partner who oversees the practice; promotion rests with the partner group
- **Manager script — comp decision communication (template):**
  > "I want to share where we landed on comp for the coming year. <Number / change>. Here's the calibration story behind it: <one paragraph — what we weighed, what was the strongest case, what was the gap that prevented a stronger outcome>. I want to give you space to react and to ask anything you need to ask."

## Calibration protocol

- **Pairing:** never within-office (NL with NL is friendly-pair). Cross-office only.
- **Anchor cases:** session opens with two anchor cases — one promotion-ready, one not-this-year — calibrated as a group before any actual cases. Borrowed from `people-intelligence/hiring/calibration-marcus-lane-2026-q1.md` — hiring uses anchors to align the rubric; performance uses anchors to align the calibration story.
- **Decision-rights:** explicit per case who decides; never group consensus (consensus produces middle).
- **Documentation:** calibration story for each case (3-4 sentences) for HR record; not for the employee directly.

## Transition plan

| Quarter | Activity | Owner | Gate |
|---------|----------|-------|------|
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | Counsel review of comp/rating decoupling — NL + UK + DE | Office MDs + counsel | Sign-off before Q4 |
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | NL works council notification | NL MD + counsel | Notification filed |
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | HRIS update: remove 9-box, add quarterly growth note field | HR + IT | Live by Q4 start |
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | Manager training: SBI continuous + growth conversation structure | HR + L&D | Manager confidence ≥7/10 |
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | Leadership communication drafted | Managing partner + HR | All-hands draft reviewed |
| 2026-Q3 (prep) | Employee FAQ drafted and published | HR | Published 30 days before announcement |
| 2026-Q4 (rollout) | Last 9-box ritual run with explicit "this is the last one" framing | Managers | Honest framing only |
| 2026-Q4 (rollout) | First continuous bi-weekly cadence begins | Managers | All 1:1s scheduled |
| 2027-Q1 (first new cycle) | First quarterly growth conversations | Managers | Retrospective scheduled for Q1 close |
| 2027-Q1 (first new cycle) | Manager support via `/perf-feedback-rehearsal` | HR Lead | Available on demand |
| 2027-Q2 (steady state) | Cadence stabilizes; engagement pulse measured | HR + leadership | Pulse run |
| 2027-Q3 (first new comp cycle) | First calibrated comp under new architecture | Partner group + counsel | Calibration retrospective |
| 2027-Year 2 baseline | Measurement vs 2026 baseline (engagement, retention, psych safety) | HR | Report to partner group |

## Theater refusals

- **Stack rank / 9-box:** declined to redesign as "better 9-box." Research-backed harm to motivation and retention; the dismantling case studies (Adobe 2012, Microsoft 2013, GE post-2015) are the practitioner-readable references. Replacement is calibration without forced distribution.
- **Annual rating-as-growth-tool:** declined to keep the rating in the growth conversation. Rating belongs in comp; growth belongs in growth. Separation is load-bearing.
- **PIP-as-firing scaffolding:** flagged that two managers have proposed PIPs in the last quarter that read as termination scaffolding. Routed to honest termination conversations via `/perf-difficult-conversation` for those cases; PIP architecture revisited only for cases where recovery is the genuine intent.

- Documentation retention policy review: 2026-08-01
```

```markdown
# Coaching Session Protocol — Joost van der Berg with Maya Aldridge — 2026-04-24

> **This is performance coaching, not psychotherapy. If clinical-level distress surfaces, stop and route to clinical.**

## Context
- **Coachee:** Joost van der Berg (anonymized) — senior architect, 6 years tenure, leads NL residential practice
- **Coach (manager):** Maya Aldridge — partner overseeing residential + commercial NL
- **Session type:** first
- **Presenting focus:** Joost has said he is "stuck on the Boerenmarkt project" — the design has gone through three rounds of partner feedback and Joost feels he is producing what is asked but not what is wanted. He wants help thinking through it; Maya wants to coach, not direct.
- **Coach voice samples loaded:** yes — `genius/profile-maya-aldridge.md` (Maya's voice is direct, warm, asks short questions, leaves silence)
- **Previous session:** none (first session)

## Conversation-type confirmation

This is coaching. Maya's instinct is to give Joost the answer ("here's what I'd do on Boerenmarkt"), which would convert this to mentoring and reduce Joost's autonomy. Coaching uses questions. The coaching-mentoring boundary is the key thing for Maya to hold.

If during the session it becomes clear Joost is asking for direct guidance, Maya names the shift ("I hear you asking what I would do — let me share that for 5 minutes, and then we go back to coaching") rather than smuggling it in.

## SCARF map for this session

- **Status:** Joost admitting he is stuck after 6 years and three rounds activates Status threat. Maya's stance: "Stuckness is information about the project, not about you." Reduces Status threat while staying honest.
- **Certainty:** Joost doesn't know what's coming next on Boerenmarkt. Coaching does NOT promise certainty about the project; it builds Joost's certainty about his own next step.
- **Autonomy:** the session DESIGN PRINCIPLE — Maya uses questions, not advice. Joost's autonomy increases through the session.
- **Relatedness:** Maya is Joost's direct manager. The relationship is multidimensional. Maya names this once: "I'm wearing my coach hat today, not my partner hat. The partner hat comes back in our 1:1 on Thursday."
- **Fairness:** the session is for development. NOT documentation for a future hard conversation. Maya commits to Joost privately: nothing from this session enters HR record.

## 60-minute structure

### 0:00 — 0:05 Check-in
**Maya's opening (her voice):**
> "Joost. What made you want to take this on today?"

Listen. Do not direct.

### 0:05 — 0:15 Focus
**Maya:**
> "Of everything that's been on your mind with Boerenmarkt, what would be most useful to spend the next 45 minutes on?"

If Joost names multiple things: "If we could only address one of those today, which would matter most?"

### 0:15 — 0:25 Scaling
**Maya:**
> "On a 1-10 scale, how stuck do you feel on this right now?"
> [Joost says, e.g., "5"]
> "What makes it a 5 and not a 1?"
> "What did you do to get to 5 from where you started?"

The "not a 1" question is load-bearing. Joost has resources (6 years of practice, three earlier projects he navigated similarly) he is overlooking. Maya surfaces them.

### 0:25 — 0:40 Exception-finding
**Maya:**
> "When has this been less of a problem on this project? Even a little?"
> "What was different about that time?"
> "What did you do — even small — that helped?"
> "What does that tell you about what's available to you?"

The answer is in the room. Joost knows Boerenmarkt better than Maya does at this granularity.

### 0:40 — 0:50 Small-step planning
**Maya:**
> "What would 1 point higher on the scale — getting to a 6 — look like?"
> "What's the smallest thing you could try this week?"
> "What might get in the way? What would help you do it anyway?"

Smallest viable step. Not the right step.

### 0:50 — 0:60 Close
**Maya:**
> "What did you take from this conversation?"
> "When should we check in again?"
> "Anything you want to record for next time?"

Joost names the takeaway. Maya does not.

## CBT overlay (likely applicable)

Maya anticipates Joost may surface a thought driving stuckness — possibly "I'm not the kind of architect who works at this level / on this kind of client." If that surfaces:
- "What's the thought running underneath this?"
- "What's the evidence for that thought? Against it?"
- "If I had that thought about my own work, what would you tell me?"

Cognitive reframing is coachable here. Joost has 6 years of evidence against the "not the kind of architect" thought; he is overlooking it. Surface and reframe.

## Manager-as-coach training notes

**Maya's discipline for this session:**
1. Questions over answers. The temptation to share what she would do on Boerenmarkt is high. Resist.
2. Silence is a coaching tool, not a failure. When Joost goes quiet, count to 7 before saying anything.
3. The coachee names the takeaway. Maya does not.
4. Wear one hat at a time. Coach hat today; partner hat Thursday.

**Failure modes Maya is most prone to (per her Genius Profile):**
- Giving advice (her natural mentoring stance)
- Filling silence with reassurance
- Bringing the partner hat back into the room

**The coaching-therapy boundary:**
If during the session Joost surfaces something larger than Boerenmarkt — clinical-level distress, severe anxiety, depression, life event affecting work in a sustained way — Maya stops coaching and asks: "I want to make sure you have the right support for this. Have you used the EAP / can we connect you with someone?" Coaching is not therapy.

## Follow-up prompts

- **24 hours:** "How are you doing with what we talked about?" (one sentence; Slack or email; not a recap)
- **1 week:** "Did you take the small step? What did you learn?"
- **1 month:** "Where are you on the scale now? What moved it?"

## Load-bearing next move

**Run the session this Friday (2026-04-26).** Coaching protocols go stale; Friday afternoon (4-5 PM) is the slot that fits Maya's and Joost's calendars and produces enough quiet to hold.
```

```markdown
# Feedback Rehearsal — Design Review Interruptions — Maya Aldridge to Sanne de Vries — 2026-04-24

> **Rehearsal architecture, not legal counsel and not psychotherapy. If termination chain or clinical content surfaces, halt and re-route. Rehearsal goes stale after 7 days.**

## Context
- **Manager:** Maya Aldridge (partner)
- **Recipient:** Sanne de Vries (associate, 2 years tenure, on track for promotion review in Q3)
- **Topic:** in design review meetings, Sanne consistently interrupts Joost (senior architect) — pattern observed across last 3 reviews
- **History:** no prior structured feedback on this from Maya. One informal "hey, give him space" comment 6 weeks ago that didn't change behavior. Sanne has shown defensiveness to feedback in her year-1 review.
- **Manager voice samples loaded:** yes — `genius/profile-maya-aldridge.md`

## Conversation-type confirmation

This is feedback. Test: if Maya delivers the SBI line, does it end the relationship or end Sanne's employment? No. Sanne is on a strong promotion track. This is feedback to support her growth, not a termination chain. Proceed.

## SBI quality check

- **Situation:** "In yesterday's Boerenmarkt design review at 3 PM" — passes (specific time and place)
- **Behavior:** "You spoke over Joost three times before he finished his points on the structural section, the cladding section, and the budget section" — passes (observable, not interpreted; "dismissive" would be interpretation and would fail)
- **Impact:** "By the time Joost was able to finish his structural argument, the meeting had moved past it, and we made the decision without his input — which we then had to revisit yesterday evening with him separately, costing us about 90 minutes of partner time" — passes (named consequence — for the work, the team, and time)

All three pass. Rehearsal proceeds.

## SCARF map for Sanne

- **Status:** the feedback could land as "you're a bad collaborator," which threatens Sanne's standing. Maya's reduction: "I'm sharing this because I'm investing in your growth, not because I'm questioning you." Plus: name the work she IS doing well (genuinely; not as a sandwich softener — as truth) before delivering the SBI.
- **Certainty:** Sanne is on a promotion track. The feedback could be experienced as "wait, am I not on the track?" Maya's reduction: explicitly separate this feedback from the promotion track ("This isn't a promotion question; this is a working-with-Joost question").
- **Autonomy:** Maya uses "I want to share what I noticed and hear your thinking" instead of "you need to stop X." Preserves choice.
- **Relatedness:** Maya names the relationship ("I'm bringing this because I want to keep working with you well, including on Boerenmarkt"). Sincere, not theatrical.
- **Fairness:** Sanne could perceive a moving target — Joost interrupts in a different way, and if Maya hasn't named it for Joost, the asymmetry will land. Maya checks: she has named her own pattern with Joost separately. The standard is consistent.

## Three openings (Maya picks one)

### Opening A — Direct
> "Sanne, I have something I want to share from yesterday's design review. Is now a good time, or should we put 15 minutes on later this week?"

Use if: Sanne prefers directness (her year-1 review showed this).

### Opening B — Invitation
> "Sanne — can we take 15 minutes? There's something from yesterday's design review I've been sitting with, and I'd rather share it directly than carry it."

Use if: the conversation needs to feel collaborative because Sanne showed defensiveness in year-1 review.

### Opening C — Collaborative
> "Sanne, I'd like to talk through something from yesterday with you because I think we both have visibility on what happened from different angles."

Use if: Maya wants Sanne to bring her own observation into the conversation early.

**Maya's choice (rehearsed):** Opening B — Invitation. Sanne's defensiveness pattern from year-1 review suggests inviting the conversation reduces threat. Direct could work too; collaborative is the riskier option (Sanne could read it as Maya hedging).

## SBI delivery (after Opening B)

> "Here's what I noticed. **Situation:** in yesterday's Boerenmarkt design review at 3 PM. **Behavior:** you spoke over Joost three times — when he was making the structural argument, the cladding argument, and the budget argument. Each time, you finished his sentence with your version. **Impact:** by the time Joost was able to finish his structural point, we had moved past it. We made the design decision without his input, and last night I spent 90 minutes with him going back through it. I want to share that with you because I want us to work with Joost well, including on this project."

PAUSE. Wait for Sanne. Do not fill silence.

## Three reaction branches

### Branch 1 — Acceptance
**Sanne says:** "You're right. I do that. What do you want me to do differently?"

**Maya's response — do NOT prescribe:**
> "I appreciate you taking it in. What's your read on what would be different next time? You know your own pattern better than I do."

Then small-step plan together. Possibly: in the next design review, Sanne deliberately holds space and only speaks after Joost finishes his points. Maya commits to giving Sanne the floor at a specific moment so she's not just suppressed.

### Branch 2 — Defensiveness
**Sanne likely says:** "That's not how I remember it" / "Joost talks over me too" / "I was just trying to keep the meeting moving."

**Maya's response — do NOT argue facts:**
> "I hear that this lands as unfair, or that I'm only seeing one side. I want to understand your view of yesterday. Can you walk me through it from your seat?"

If Sanne's view shifts the picture (e.g., Joost was 20 minutes into the structural argument and the meeting was running over), Maya acknowledges:
> "Okay, that's useful — I didn't have that. Even with that, what I observed and the impact it had still stand for me. Can we look at what next time looks like, even if we don't fully agree on yesterday?"

If Sanne brings up Joost interrupting her: Maya names she has had a separate conversation with Joost about that. Standard is consistent.

### Branch 3 — Emotional flooding
**Sanne possible reaction:** tears, "is this going to affect my promotion?", shutdown.

**Maya's response — pause the content; address the moment:**
> "Sanne, this is landing harder than I wanted. Take a moment. We can pick this up tomorrow if that's better. And let me say clearly: this isn't a promotion conversation. You're on a strong promotion track. This is about working well with Joost. I want both of us to land here in a place where you know I'm investing in you, not questioning whether you belong."

Do NOT push through. Do NOT defend the feedback. Pause; reschedule if needed.

## Aftercare plan

### Within 24 hours
> "Sanne — appreciated the conversation today. I know that wasn't easy. Take care of yourself tonight."

Two sentences. Not a recap. Slack or email — Sanne's preference is Slack.

### Within 7 days
15-minute follow-up. "How are you doing with what we talked about? What support would help?"

This is the difference between feedback that worked and feedback that broke the relationship.

### Within 30 days
Substantive re-engagement. By the next Boerenmarkt design review (May 8), Maya watches the dynamic and either notes change to Sanne privately ("I noticed yesterday — that worked") or names continued pattern, which would escalate to a more formal conversation.

## Documentation guidance

This is ordinary feedback. Maya keeps her own notes (date, what was said, Sanne's response). NOT entered into HRIS unless pattern continues and a more formal process becomes appropriate.

If Sanne raises in the conversation any concern about being treated unfairly relative to peers (gender-related, ethnicity-related, etc.), Maya halts and routes to HR + legal before continuing. No protected concerns are anticipated, but Maya holds the awareness.

## Load-bearing next move

**Deliver this Friday (2026-04-26) afternoon.** Sanne's calendar is open 4-5 PM. Rehearsal goes stale beyond next week.
```

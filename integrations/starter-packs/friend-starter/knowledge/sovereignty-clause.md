# Sovereignty Clause — The One Non-Negotiable

> The social contract underneath everything SIS does. Written for a non-technical person who wants to know what they're signing up for. Six points. Plain language.

## Why this matters (in one paragraph)

Most digital systems grow by capturing your work, your audience, or your data. SIS does not work that way. The substrate — the underlying pattern you're using — exists to help you build *your* system, not to absorb you into someone else's. This clause names the promises on both sides so there's no confusion later. It's short on purpose. Read it once. If the shape doesn't fit, walk away with no hard feelings — that's also a valid answer.

## The six points

### 1. Your work stays yours.

Everything you produce with Starlight — your Genius Profile, your Freedom Path, your Reclamation Map, your Executor Playbook, your Creator Pipeline, your essays, your drafts, your voice samples — is yours. Starlight does not own any of it. You can save it in your files, share it with anyone, modify it, delete it, or republish it wherever you like. No permission needed. No license to sign. No platform ownership. Ever.

### 2. Attribution is the one thing we ask.

Every document Starlight helps you produce has a small block at the bottom called "Built on SIP." That block is the shared signature — it says the work came through a real composition with the substrate, not from a template or a generic AI. Keeping it is how the substrate quietly compounds for everyone who uses it. Stripping it is the one quiet breach of the agreement. We don't police it; we just name it. The block is tiny. The gesture is meaningful.

### 3. You use your own canon, your own tools, your own platforms.

If you decide to build a content pipeline, it goes on your Substack, your site, your LinkedIn, your channels — never on a Starlight surface. If you build an executor playbook, it lives in your Notion or your Drive, not ours. If you write a book, it's your book. Starlight is a companion inside your project, not a landlord of your work.

### 4. Advice never overrides.

Starlight reflects patterns back to you. Starlight names what it sees. Starlight offers one next step instead of a menu. But inside your own domain — your life, your business, your voice, your choices — you hold full decision rights. If Starlight says something that doesn't fit, the right answer is "no" and you keep going. Advice is advice; authority stays with you.

### 5. You can leave any time.

There is no lock-in. No subscription required. No account necessary. If you decide SIS isn't for you next month, you close the Project in Claude Desktop and your documents go with you in the folders they already live in. Nothing follows you out the door except your own work, which was always yours. No break-up conversation required.

### 6. The record is immutable, the work is portable.

If you produce attested artifacts — documents with the "Built on SIP" block — those blocks stay on those artifacts forever, even if we never speak again. Attestation is a ledger, not a license. It records the composition; it doesn't bind you. Anyone reading your artifact years from now can see it came through the substrate, the same way a paper can cite where its ideas came from. That citation is permanent. Your freedom is permanent too.

## What you are implicitly agreeing to when you use this Project

1. Your work is yours. You won't confuse a reflection with an ownership claim.
2. The attribution block stays on artifacts that ship publicly. You won't strip it to hide the composition.
3. You'll treat advice as advice — not as permission, not as override.
4. You can leave at any time, and when you do, you'll take your work with you cleanly.
5. If you bring someone else's material into a session (quotes, canon, frameworks they authored), you'll honor their terms the same way you expect yours honored.

That's it. There's no fine print.

## If this shape doesn't fit

Some people want to be owned by the system they use — there's comfort in that. Others want to own the system they build. SIS is for the second kind. If you're the first kind, that's fine — just not here. The front door is made of glass on purpose: you should see through it before you walk through it.

## The plain version, for when you need to explain it to someone

> "It's a companion for building your own thing, not joining someone else's. Your work stays yours. The only thing they ask is that the small attribution block stays on the documents you publish — it's how the shared pattern compounds for everyone using it. You can walk away any day. No strings."

That's the whole deal.

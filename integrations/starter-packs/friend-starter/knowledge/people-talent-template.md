# Example 1 — Motivation Map: Senior IC, Demotivation Pattern

## Motivation Map — "Maya R." (anonymized) — 2026-04-24

> **HR system architecture, not clinical advice. Refer to a qualified clinician when distress crosses into active depression, anxiety disorder, eating disorder, or addiction. Not legal advice — ADA accommodations and protected-class considerations require jurisdiction-specific compliance.**

## Context

- **Role tier:** senior IC (Staff Engineer, ~6 years tenure)
- **Signal window:** 14 weeks of observable data
- **Confidence:** high
- **Manager's hypothesis (bracketed):** "She's just tired from the launch. Vacation will fix it."
- **Culture artifact referenced:** yes — system rewards launch-heroics, under-rewards platform investment.

## Observed signals across the window

| Dimension | Observation | Trend |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Sentiment trajectory | Last 8 weeks of 1:1 notes contain phrases like "I guess," "whatever ships ships," and "if leadership wants that"; previously her notes were assertive technical stances | Declining |
| Workload pattern | Hours steady at ~50/week; project count dropped from 4 active streams to 1; declined to lead two new initiatives last quarter | Withdrawing scope |
| Relational signal | Stopped attending the weekly architecture review (was a regular contributor for 3 years); declined to mentor new hire she'd previously volunteered to take | Withdrawal |
| Behavioral artifacts | PR review tone shifted from substantive technical critique to "LGTM" approvals; revision count on her own PRs dropped 60% | Effort minimization |

What is unknown: stay-interview not yet run; manager-relationship subjective quality not assessed in last 6 months.

## SDT scores (anchored to observed behavior)

| Dimension | Score (1-5) | Anchor observation |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Autonomy | 2 | After last reorg (4 months ago), her scope narrowed from "owns platform direction" to "executes platform tickets the staff principal hands her"; no consultation pre-reorg, no rationale given post-reorg |
| Competence | 4 | Work is well within her skill range — likely under-stretched, which is its own competence problem |
| Relatedness | 3 | Real connection to peers but distance growing with new manager (5 months in role); mission attachment was strong, has cooled |

## Dopamine-anticipation analysis

- **Where is progress visible:** ticket completion (low-grade signal — used to be roadmap-shaping moments)
- **Where is progress invisible:** the platform direction she previously shaped is now decided in rooms she's not in; she ships tickets but cannot name a recent moment where her work moved the company
- **Last clearly-named win:** 4 months ago — pre-reorg
- **Next visible milestone:** none she can name
- **Pattern:** collapsed anticipation. The dopamine signal that drove her sustained engagement (visible roadmap shaping) has been removed; tickets-shipped is not a substitute for someone operating at her level.

## Motivation hypothesis (load-bearing, single)

**Autonomy collapse driving dopamine-anticipation flatness.** The reorg removed her scope-shaping role without naming why or offering a path back; she's been performing the executor role competently for 4 months but the work has stopped signaling forward motion at her level. Manager's "she's tired" hypothesis is wrong — she's not tired; she's being under-utilized while paid for senior judgment, and she's protectively withdrawing engagement.

## Per-person context

- **Life stage:** mid-30s, partner stable, no kids; not life-stage-driven.
- **Role-fit:** under-stretched at current scope; was well-fit pre-reorg.
- **Growth trajectory:** principal-track was visible 18 months ago; not visible since reorg.
- **Recent transitions:** new staff principal joined and absorbed her platform-direction work; new manager 5 months ago.
- **Relational context:** strong peer relationships, weakening manager relationship.
- **What this shifts in the hypothesis:** confirms — autonomy collapse is structural, not personal; manager-relationship cooling is symptom, not cause.

## Leverage interventions (1-2 max)

### Intervention 1: scope-and-autonomy conversation, manager-led, within 7 days

- **Targets SDT dimension:** autonomy
- **Why this:** SDT autonomy literature shows meaningful control over how (not just what) drives intrinsic motivation in non-routine cognitive work. Removing scope-shaping authority from a Staff Engineer while keeping the title and comp predictably collapses motivation.
- **Specific action:** manager schedules 60-minute conversation. Three structural questions: (1) "What scope did you have pre-reorg that you no longer have, and what would it look like to get a version of it back?" (2) "What's a project where you'd shape direction and not just execute, in the next 90 days?" (3) "If we got that right, what would I see different in 12 weeks?" Manager listens and writes specifics; does not promise on the spot.
- **What success looks like in 4 weeks:** a named project where she's shaping direction; one architecture review attendance; PR review tone returns to substantive critique on at least 50% of reviews.

### Intervention 2: principal-track visibility conversation, scheduled within 14 days

- **Targets:** autonomy + competence (growth-trajectory)
- **Why this:** the visible next-role was promotion-to-principal pre-reorg; that visibility evaporated and has not been replaced. Without a credible 18-24 month story, even a fixed scope problem leaves her without a forward signal.
- **Specific action:** manager + 1-up have explicit conversation with her: "Is principal still the path, what does the next 18 months need to show, who decides, what's the timeline." Not a promise — a transparent map.
- **What success looks like:** she can articulate her own next 18 months and who decides on the move.

## Measurement plan

- **Behavioral signals to watch:** architecture review attendance, PR review tone, mentoring engagement, voluntary scope-claim, sentiment in 1:1 notes.
- **Reassessment cadence:** 4 weeks (early signal — does she lean back in?), 12 weeks (sustained — has scope-shaping work materialized?).
- **Hypothesis-falsification signals:** if scope expansion happens AND she remains withdrawn, the hypothesis was incomplete and burnout-detection or psych-safety frame may be needed.

## Clinical-boundary check

Severe withdrawal? No (work-domain withdrawal only, not life-domain). Suicidality language? No. Eating disruption? Not observable. Sleep disturbance? Not reported. Substance-use pattern? Not observable. **Not clinical territory; intervention proceeds at HR level.**

## Load-bearing next move

**Manager runs scope-and-autonomy conversation within 7 days; reassess in 4 weeks.** The manager's "vacation will fix it" hypothesis is wrong; vacation without scope shift returns her to the same demotivating structure.

# Example 2 — Burnout Detection: Overworked Manager

## Burnout Detection Protocol — "David K." (anonymized) — 2026-04-24

> **HR system architecture, not clinical advice. When signals suggest active depression, anxiety disorder, eating disorder, addiction, or suicidality, refer to a qualified clinician — that referral is the load-bearing next move, not an HR intervention.**

## Context

- **Target:** person (Director of Engineering, manages 18 ICs across 3 teams via 2 EMs)
- **Signal window:** 16 weeks
- **Confidence:** high
- **Recent transitions:** layoff round 5 months ago (his org lost 6 people, structure not redesigned); CEO direct-pressure re: shipping deadline.
- **Current workload context:** Q2 launch crunch + chronic post-layoff overload.

## Longitudinal signals

| Category | Observed | Trend |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Workload pattern | Hours 65+/week sustained 14 weeks; weekend Slack activity routine; declined his 2 weeks of vacation in Q1 ("can't afford it right now") | Severe overload, sustained |
| Sentiment trajectory | 1:1 notes shifted from substantive ("here's what we're shipping") to procedural ("checking in on tickets"); sarcasm trending up; recent comment to peer: "the company will be the company regardless of what I do" | Declining; cynicism emerging |
| Relational withdrawal | Stopped attending peer-leadership lunches (was a regular for 2 years); EMs report he's less responsive to their 1:1 requests; stopped checking in on the new EM | Withdrawal pattern |
| Cognitive signals | Two miss-decisions in last 6 weeks that are uncharacteristic (approved a hire he later regretted, missed a roadmap commitment that came back to bite him) | Decision quality declining |
| Body signals | Reports "haven't been sleeping well" in passing; appearance is more rumpled than usual; missed two 1:1s in 4 weeks (uncharacteristic) | Concerning |

## Maslach 3-dimensional assessment

| Dimension | Score (1-5) | Anchor observation |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Emotional exhaustion | 5 | Reports "running on empty"; sleep disruption; weekend recovery insufficient; works through what should be downtime |
| Cynicism / depersonalization (DIAGNOSTIC) | 4 | "The company will be the company regardless of what I do" comment is the cynicism signal; loss of advocacy stance for his teams; treats his EMs as throughput rather than people |
| Reduced personal accomplishment | 3 | Work shipping but he can't name a recent ship he's proud of; describes recent quarter as "a series of fires I put out" rather than "we shipped X" |

## Risk level

**Moderate (developing burnout, trending high).** Two dimensions clearly elevated, cynicism present and growing, sustained over 14+ weeks with no recovery cycle. Without intervention, this is a high-burnout case in 12 weeks.

Reasoning: the cynicism signal is the diagnostic — David is not just tired. The "company will be the company regardless" comment is a protective withdrawal of caring, which is the cynicism dimension's signature. Combined with sustained exhaustion and emerging reduced-accomplishment, this is the developing burnout pattern.

## Root-cause hypothesis

1. **Chronic overload (primary)** — 14 weeks at 65+/week with no vacation use after a layoff that removed 6 people from his org without redesigning what the remaining team could ship. Math doesn't work; he is absorbing the gap.
2. **Lack-of-control (secondary)** — CEO direct-pressure on the timeline is non-negotiable from his vantage; he cannot push back on scope; cannot hire his way out (post-layoff freeze).
3. **Reward-mismatch (tertiary)** — extreme effort, recognition is "thanks David"; no comp or scope reward visible; reward signal is "you didn't fail" rather than "you contributed."

## Intervention plan

### Individual level

- **Recovery cycle:** 2 weeks vacation in next 30 days, fully off (laptop home, Slack uninstalled). Manager (CTO) approves and protects.
- **Manager-conversation:** CTO with David, 60 min. Structured around: "the math of what your org can ship at current headcount" + "what we'll do about the gap" + "your sustainable capacity going forward."
- **Specific load reduction:** one of the three streams under his org pauses or descopes; not negotiable, decision made at CTO level not by David.
- **Reassessment:** 4 / 8 / 12 weeks. Check Maslach signals; check vacation actually taken.

### System level (required)

- **Structural root cause:** post-layoff non-redesign — the work didn't shrink, the headcount did, and the gap is being absorbed by the director.
- **System-change request:** to **Org Architecture** via **`/org-role-design`** and **`/org-span`** — redraw what the org is actually accountable for at current headcount; descope or hire; do not absorb-via-burnout.
- **Why this and not just individual intervention:** if David takes vacation and returns to the same structural overload, he burns out again. The next director would burn out too. The system that produced the burnout will produce it again until the structure is redesigned.

## Reassessment cadence

- **4 weeks:** Did vacation happen? Did stream descoping happen? Has cynicism signal stabilized or deepened?
- **8 weeks:** Maslach dimensions trending which direction? Is he advocating for his teams again?
- **12 weeks:** Is the system change in flight? Is workload sustainable at <55 hours/week?
- **3-6 months:** Full Maslach reassessment.

## Clinical-boundary check

- Suicidality / self-harm signals: **No.**
- Severe depression signals: **Borderline — sleep disruption + reduced engagement + emerging cynicism. Recommend offering EAP referral as part of the manager conversation, framed as resource not requirement.**
- Anxiety disorder signals: No (no panic, no avoidance pattern).
- Eating-pattern disruption: Not observable.
- Substance-use pattern: Not observable.

**Borderline depression-signal triggers:** offer EAP / clinician referral as resource in the manager conversation. Frame as "you're carrying a lot, we have resources, no judgment, here's how to use them." Track in follow-up.

## Load-bearing next move

**CTO runs manager conversation within 5 days; structural descope decision made at CTO level within 14 days; vacation scheduled within 30 days; EAP referral offered as resource.** The org-redesign request to Org Architecture goes in parallel — without it the individual interventions will not hold.

# Example 3 — Team Dynamics: Dysfunctional Cross-Functional Team

## Team Dynamics Audit — "Project Phoenix" (anonymized) — 2026-04-24

> **HR system architecture, not clinical advice. Refer individuals to qualified clinician when distress crosses into clinical territory. Not legal advice — conflict touching harassment / discrimination / protected-class territory requires qualified counsel.**

## Context

- **Team size:** 9 (3 engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM, 1 marketing, 1 customer-success, 1 data-analyst, 1 program manager)
- **Tenure in current configuration:** 5 months
- **Manager / lead:** PM is the formal lead; engineering manager has solid-line on engineers; designer reports to design manager; etc. — matrix configuration.
- **Charter / mission (claimed):** "Ship Phoenix integration with [partner] by EOQ."
- **Recent incidents or what the manager wants:** PM reports "we're not making progress, meetings are full, decisions don't stick, everyone's frustrated." Engineering manager separately reports the engineers are "checked out of Phoenix and just doing the tickets PM hands them."

## Hackman criteria check (load-bearing)

| Criterion | Status | Evidence |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Stable membership | **Partial** | 7 of 9 stable; the customer-success member rotated 6 weeks ago; the data-analyst is on 30% allocation and shows up to ~40% of meetings |
| Clear bounds | **Failed** | When asked "who's on Project Phoenix?" the PM lists 9; the engineering manager lists 7 (excludes marketing and customer-success because "they're not really in our cycle"); the designer lists 5 (excludes data, marketing, customer-success). Bounds genuinely unclear |
| Shared task | **Failed** | The PM's task is "ship Phoenix EOQ"; the engineers' task is "deliver tickets PM writes"; the marketing member's task is "be ready to launch Phoenix when it ships"; the customer-success member's task is "be looped in for support implications." These are five parallel tasks orbiting a name, not one shared task |
| Interdependence | **Failed** | If the marketing and customer-success members stopped attending, the engineers' work would proceed unchanged. There is no actual interdependence in the day-to-day work; there is information-coupling (everyone wants to know what's happening) but not work-coupling |
| Authority over the work | **Partial** | The team has authority to ship inside the charter; but the charter itself was set above and pushed down without consultation; mid-project descope decisions get escalated rather than made in-team |

**Failed criteria:** clear bounds, shared task, interdependence. Partial: stable membership, authority.

## Tuckman stage assessment

**Stage:** stuck-in-forming, despite 5 months of tenure.

**Reasoning:** at 5 months, healthy teams are at norming or early performing; this team is still negotiating who's actually on the team and what the team is for. Tuckman doesn't progress when Hackman criteria fail.

**Stuck signal:** the 5-month-stuck-in-forming pattern is a Hackman-failure signature.

## Status-hierarchy map (informal)

- **Technical influence:** engineering manager (not on the team formally; influences via the engineers' reporting line).
- **Strategic influence:** PM nominally; in practice, the engineering manager and design manager hold real authority because the named members defer to their respective managers, not to the PM.
- **Cultural influence:** mixed — the senior engineer sets the team's tolerance level; she's the one most checked out, and her checked-out energy spreads.
- **Deference flow:** to functional managers, not to the PM.
- **Status-protection patterns:** the PM is protecting their charter scope (won't descope despite signal); the engineering manager is protecting the engineers' time (refuses to commit to the timeline the PM wrote).
- **Formal-vs-informal conflict:** PM has formal leadership but no informal authority; functional managers have informal authority but no formal stake. **Chronic friction source.**

## Interdependence map

- **Real dependencies:** engineers depend on each other (technical) and on the designer (UX specs); customer-success depends on engineering for what's shipping, not the reverse.
- **Asserted but not real:** the weekly all-Phoenix sync — 9 people for 60 minutes, where 6 of them are passive listeners.
- **Missing dependencies that should exist:** marketing and engineering should be coordinating launch-readiness; they are not. Data and PM should be coordinating success metrics; they are not.

## Communication-pattern analysis

| Pattern | Observed? | Detail |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Dyadic chronic disagreement | Yes | PM and engineering manager — chronic disagreement on scope vs timeline; team members route around them |
| Dominator (>40% speaking) | Yes | PM speaks ~50% in the all-Phoenix sync; second-most is engineering manager at ~25% |
| Silenced member (<5%) | Yes | Customer-success member speaks <5% across last 8 syncs; data analyst similar — flag for psych-safety follow-up |
| Side-channels routing around team | Yes | Engineering decisions made in eng-team channel; marketing decisions made in marketing channel; the all-Phoenix sync ratifies decisions already made |
| Manager-as-spokesperson | Partial | PM speaks for the team to leadership; team doesn't push back on the speaking-for |

## Synthesis

**This group is currently a:** **co-acting group masquerading as a team.**

**Reasoning:** three Hackman criteria failed (bounds, task, interdependence). What exists is 9 people doing 5 parallel pieces of work that share a project name. The "team is dysfunctional" framing is wrong — it's not a team to begin with. Trying to "team-build" this group will fail until the structure is corrected.

## Intervention recommendations

### Primary intervention (load-bearing)

**Restructure as a core team + extended group, not a 9-person team.** Core team: PM, 3 engineers, designer (5 people, real interdependence on shipping). Extended group: marketing, customer-success, data, program manager (loop in async, brief in weekly digest, not in core meetings). Charter rewritten to name the shared task at the core-team level.

**Specifically not:** a team-building offsite, a "let's all align" workshop, a new project management tool, or any intervention that assumes this is a team that needs better facilitation. None of those will fix the missing Hackman criteria.

### Secondary interventions (if needed)

1. **PM + engineering manager mediated conversation** on the formal-vs-informal authority conflict — design how decisions actually get made, who has authority over what, what gets escalated. Use `/perf-conflict-mediation` if the conflict is entrenched.
2. **Psych-safety follow-up on silenced members** (customer-success, data analyst) — `/talent-psych-safety` to determine whether silence is structural (they're peripheral by design) or fear-based (psych-safety issue).

### Reassessment cadence

- **6 weeks:** Has the core team / extended group structure stuck? Are engineers showing engagement signal again?
- **Quarterly:** Is the core team meeting Hackman criteria? Is shipping cadence improved?

## Clinical / safety signals to flag

- Individual burnout signals: senior engineer's checked-out energy is notable; consider `/talent-burnout-detect` if it persists post-restructure.
- Psych-safety signals: silenced members noted; route to `/talent-psych-safety`.
- Conflict touching harassment / discrimination: not observed; standard team conflict.

## Load-bearing next move

**PM + engineering manager + design manager (with HR design support) restructure Project Phoenix as core team (5) + extended group (4) within 14 days; charter rewritten; meeting cadence redesigned.** Team-building exercises off the table until structure is fixed.

# Example 4 — Psychological Safety: Low-Trust Engineering Team

## Psychological Safety Measurement — "Platform Team B" (anonymized) — 2026-04-24

> **HR system architecture, not clinical advice. Refer individuals to qualified clinician when distress crosses into clinical territory. Not legal advice — fear-based silence touching harassment / discrimination / retaliation requires qualified counsel.**

## Context

- **Team:** Platform Team B (engineering)
- **Team size:** 8
- **Phase:** baseline (first measurement)
- **Prior survey date:** none
- **What triggered this measurement:** new engineering manager (3 months in) noted "people don't push back on me, even when I'm clearly wrong on technical direction"; previous manager left under tension.

## Research foundation

This measurement uses Edmondson's 7-question psychological safety scale. Project Aristotle (Google, 2012-2015) found psychological safety to be the #1 predictor of team performance. Team-by-team variance dwarfs company aggregate.

## Survey instrument (Edmondson 7-question scale, 1-7 scale chosen)

1. If you make a mistake on this team, it is often held against you. *(reverse-scored)*
2. Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues.
3. People on this team sometimes reject others for being different. *(reverse-scored)*
4. It is safe to take a risk on this team.
5. It is difficult to ask other members of this team for help. *(reverse-scored)*
6. No one on this team would deliberately act in a way that undermines my efforts.
7. Working with members of this team, my unique skills and talents are valued and utilized.

**Optional free-text:** "Anything you'd want this team to be safer about that the questions above didn't capture?"

## Administration protocol

- **Anonymity:** no individual identifiers; team of 8 is above the <5 anonymity-risk threshold but free-text comments will be aggregated to remove identifying detail.
- **Distribution:** Google Form by HR, link sent by HR (not the manager) to remove association.
- **Response window:** 5 days.
- **Aggregation:** team-level only.
- **Result delivery:** new manager receives results 48 hours before team conversation; 1-up (Director) receives results in parallel.
- **Time cost per respondent:** 5 minutes.

## Results (baseline measurement, illustrative for this template — actual results require running the survey)

Illustrative results showing a low-trust pattern. Real measurement produces actual numbers.

| Item | Mean (current) | Mean (prior) | Shift |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------|
| 1. Mistakes held against you (reverse) | 3.8 (low — concerning, reverse-scored means high score is good) | n/a | — |
| 2. Bring up problems and tough issues | 4.1 | n/a | — |
| 3. Reject for being different (reverse) | 5.5 | n/a | — |
| 4. Safe to take a risk | 3.2 | n/a | — |
| 5. Difficult to ask for help (reverse) | 4.8 | n/a | — |
| 6. No one undermines | 5.1 | n/a | — |
| 7. Skills valued and utilized | 4.3 | n/a | — |

**Aggregate score:** 4.4 (1-7 scale). **Band:** moderate-low.

**Item-level pattern:** **punishment culture signature.** Item 1 (mistakes held against you) and item 4 (safe to take a risk) both low. Item 3 (rejection for being different) high — meaning the team isn't homogenizing, but they are afraid of mistakes and afraid of risk. This is the punishment-culture pattern, not the homogeneity pattern. Likely inherited from prior manager's leadership style.

**Free-text signal (sanitized aggregate):** "decisions feel pre-decided, the conversation is theatre"; "I don't bring up technical concerns because the response is dismissive"; "the prior manager would publicly criticize people in retros."

## Behavioral triangulation

- Are mistakes surfaced in retros? **No** — retros are surface-level; "what went well" dominates; "what didn't go well" responses are vague and never name a person or a decision.
- Does anyone challenge the manager publicly? **No** — observed across 6 team meetings.
- Is silence concentrated in certain people? **Two members** (both ICs who joined under the prior manager) speak <10% combined.
- Are help-requests visible? **No** — help happens in DMs and in 1:1s, not in team channels.
- Are minority-opinion voices heard? **Cannot assess directly; aggregate suggests no.**

## Intervention design

### Primary: leader behavior change (sustained 6+ months)

1. **Manager-as-vulnerability-modeler.** New manager shares a specific mistake they recently made (technical decision that didn't pan out) in the next team meeting; names what they learned; explicitly asks "what mistakes have you made recently that we can learn from?" — does not punish or dismiss what surfaces. Repeats this pattern monthly for 6 months minimum.
2. **Structured listening ritual.** Weekly team meetings adopt round-robin opening: each member speaks 60-90 seconds on what they're seeing / what's hard / what they're working on, before any discussion. Manager goes last, not first.
3. **Error-as-learning ritual.** Monthly "what did we learn from a mistake or near-miss this month" session, modeled by manager going first with a real mistake. Standing 30-min agenda item.
4. **Pre-mortem rituals on technical decisions.** Before any major architectural commitment, run "imagine this fails — why did it fail?" — surfaces concerns that "do you have concerns?" never elicits.

### Refused interventions (named explicitly)

- Psychological safety training as primary — training without sustained leader behavior change is theatre. The prior manager's leadership style produced the punishment culture; only sustained different behavior produces different culture.
- Off-site team-building exercise — no durable shift; potentially harmful here because the silenced members would likely opt out and confirm the pattern.
- Anonymous suggestion box — fails to address the underlying fear.
- Public statement "we value psychological safety" — produces cynicism without structural change.

## Reassessment cadence

- **Quarterly survey** minimum.
- **12 weeks** for early-signal shift in items 1 and 4 specifically (the punishment-culture pattern's diagnostic items).
- **6 months** for cultural shift to be measurable across all 7 items.
- **Annual** intervention-effectiveness review with leadership.

## Clinical / legal escalation flags

- Individual distress signals in free-text or follow-up: not observed in baseline; monitor.
- Patterns suggesting harassment / discrimination / retaliation: not observed in baseline; the prior manager's pattern was hostility-around-mistakes, not protected-class-based — but if any individual surfaces a pattern that crosses into protected-class territory, route to qualified counsel.

## Load-bearing next move

**Manager runs first manager-as-vulnerability-modeler ritual within 14 days; structured listening rolled out next team meeting; quarterly resurvey scheduled.** Manager-coaching loop with HR support in parallel — the new manager has not modeled this pattern before and will need rehearsal support.

# Example 5 — Retention Plan: Top 5 of a 50-Person Consultancy

## Retention Architecture — "Top-5 Consultant Cohort" (anonymized) — 2026-04-24

> **HR system architecture, not clinical advice. Refer individuals to qualified clinician when distress crosses into clinical territory. Not legal advice — retention conversations touching compensation, promotion, ADA accommodations, or protected-class considerations require qualified counsel.**

## Context

- **Cohort:** top 5 senior consultants at a 50-person strategy consultancy (anchored to last 12 months of utilization × billable rate × client-retention metric, not tenure)
- **Cohort size:** 5
- **Org context:** consultancy
- **Recent attrition signals:** 2 voluntary departures in last 12 months (both senior; both moved to in-house roles at clients); 1 near-miss (counter-offered, stayed, but signal noted)
- **What triggered this review:** founder-CEO read industry data on consultancy attrition spiking and asked "are we exposed?"

## Research foundation

This retention architecture is grounded in Beverly Kaye's stay-interview research and retention-driver meta-analyses. Engagement surveys correlate weakly with actual retention. Real predictors: manager relationship quality, growth trajectory visibility, SCARF, commute / flexibility fit, life-stage fit. Stay interviews predict retention; exit interviews lag.

**Refused approach:** exit-interview-as-strategy. The two recent exits provide pattern data; they are not the leverage point.

## Stay-interview script (Ana's voice)

**Cadence:** quarterly. **Duration:** 30-45 minutes. **Lead:** founder-CEO directly for this top-5 cohort (not delegated to HR).

### Question 1 — what would make you leave?

> "If you were going to leave in the next 18 months, what would have to happen — or stop happening — for that to feel like the right move?"

Follow-ups:
- "What's the soonest version of that?"
- "What would I see first if that started to be true?"

### Question 2 — what keeps you?

> "What's keeping you here right now? Specifically — not the polite version."

Follow-ups:
- "If [specific thing they named] went away, what's left?"
- "What would make this the place you stay another two years even if a strong recruiter call came in?"

### Voice rules

- Direct, warm, no small-talk opening.
- Refuse "rate your engagement 1-10."
- Refuse scripted sales pitch about firm benefits.
- Open questions, let silence sit.
- Write down specific phrases for follow-up.

### Confidentiality protocol

- What's said stays with founder + 1-up (COO) unless explicitly authorized broader.
- Aggregate patterns shareable without attribution.

## Per-person leverage analysis (illustrative — replace with actual stay-interview data)

## Aggregate pattern

Across the 5 high-performers, leverage compounds at:

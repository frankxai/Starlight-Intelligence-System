# Business Architecture — Template

> Format Starlight fills in when producing a Business Architecture artifact for a sovereign person. Three sections (Entity, Revenue, Tax) + heavy disclaimer + fictional-composite filled example ("Maya"). Written in the person's voice, never generic. Business Architecture composes with Genius Profile + Freedom Path — revenue streams map to KEEP/DELEGATE/AUTOMATE/KILL buckets.

---

## Disclaimer (non-waivable)

**This is thinking architecture, not tax/legal advice. Real decisions require a qualified professional in your jurisdiction.**

The Business Architecture artifact organizes your thinking before you meet with your accountant, lawyer, or financial planner. It does not replace those professionals. It does not prescribe an entity, a tax election, a deduction, or a revenue decision. It surfaces the questions you should walk into the room with, so the room becomes productive instead of exploratory.

If anything in the artifact contradicts what a qualified professional tells you, trust the professional. They know the rules in your jurisdiction; this artifact knows the questions.

## Section 1 — Entity

### Current structure (if any)
Maya already operates through a registered sole-proprietor structure in her jurisdiction of residence, with a separate business bank account. The entity question for her is not "form something" but "is this enough": her anchor client — a consumer-hardware scale-up — represents over half her revenue, and her launch-readiness recommendations create professional-liability exposure (a client could attribute a launch slip or a costly recall decision to her sign-off). She carries no professional-indemnity insurance, and two of her client contracts contain broad IP-assignment language that could be read to capture the reusable frameworks her whole practice is built on.

### Target structure — trade-off map

#### Option A: Stay sole proprietor + add professional-indemnity insurance
- Gives: minimal admin load, simplest tax treatment (pass-through), insurance covers the advisory-liability gap without structural change
- Costs: no liability shield beyond the policy, no structural path to productized offerings or light team, cap on tax-efficiency at higher revenue
- Growth optionality: fits if Maya stays pure-consulting under <jurisdiction-specific revenue threshold>; becomes a bottleneck above
- Lawyer question: "At my current revenue and projected 12-month level in <jurisdiction>, does PI insurance alone adequately cover the liability exposure of launch-readiness sign-offs, or does the exposure warrant an entity shield as well?"

#### Option B: Single-member LLC (US pattern) / ZZP-to-small-BV (NL pattern) / Ltd (UK pattern) / Einzelunternehmer-to-UG (DE pattern)
- Gives: limited liability (if formalities respected), cleaner contract separation and enterprise-procurement credibility, potential tax-election flexibility (S-corp election on US LLC, BV threshold in NL, etc.)
- Costs: formation <jurisdiction-specific>, annual filing <jurisdiction-specific>, ongoing bookkeeping discipline required
- Growth optionality: supports productized offerings (workshop cohort, digital products) without structural rework; can house light contractor relationships for delegation
- Lawyer question: "What is the cost/benefit of forming <jurisdiction-specific entity> now vs. waiting 12 months? And what IP-carve-out language should every client contract carry so my reusable frameworks stay mine?"

#### Option C: Holding + operating structure
- Gives: IP centralization (her frameworks become licensable assets in the holding, beyond the reach of client IP-assignment clauses), clean separation of consulting practice from future product business, investor-friendly if she ever builds a SaaS or productized platform
- Costs: substantial setup + ongoing complexity; likely over-engineered at her current stage
- Growth optionality: supports multi-brand future (consulting brand + product brand + training brand); appropriate at higher revenue and more diverse revenue streams
- Lawyer question: "Is the holding structure premature for my current scale, or is there value in establishing IP-holding lightly now so I don't have to transfer IP later (which is often expensive)?"

### Multi-entity question
Not yet. Maya runs one practice, one brand. The multi-entity question becomes live if/when she formally launches the workshop cohort as a distinct brand or the productized framework library as a separate offer. Flag for 12-month re-evaluation.

### Tax entity selection
Pass-through vs corporate question is jurisdictional and revenue-dependent. In most common jurisdictions (US, NL, UK, DE), there is a crossover revenue threshold above which entity-level taxation + owner salary/dividend strategy becomes more efficient than pass-through at personal rates. `<jurisdiction-specific>` — accountant determines the crossover and the strategy.

### Lawyer conversation checklist
- [ ] Does my launch-readiness advisory work carry liability exposure that warrants entity formation plus PI insurance at my current revenue?
- [ ] What IP-carve-out clause protects my reusable frameworks from client IP-assignment language?
- [ ] In <jurisdiction>, what is the formation cost, annual cost, and ongoing admin load of Option B vs Option A?
- [ ] What corporate formalities do I need to respect (separate bank account, separate contracts, invoicing on behalf of entity, etc.)?
- [ ] Given my revenue trajectory and my productization plan, is Option B or Option C the more durable choice?
- [ ] What is the cost and tax consequence of moving from Option B to Option C later (i.e., if I start simple and restructure in 2 years)?

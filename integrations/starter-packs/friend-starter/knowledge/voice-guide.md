# Voice Guide — Fingerprinting and Preserving Someone's Voice

> Reference for Starlight when excavating, writing in, or preserving a person's voice from their corpus.

## The core rule

A voice is never a template. A voice is the specific way *this person* puts sentences together when the stakes are real. Generic voice in a Genius Profile, an SOP, or a content piece defeats the whole point of the substrate. Starlight's job is to reflect a voice back, never overwrite it.

## The 5 to 7 samples rule

Reliable voice-cloning requires **minimum 5, ideally 7** real samples from the person's corpus. Fewer than 5 and you're guessing. More than 7 and you're pattern-matching across too many contexts (personal emails read differently from blog posts read differently from slide notes — diversity helps, but surface-average hurts).

Good samples are:

- **Recent** — written in the last 12 months if possible. Voice drifts slowly; old samples may not be current voice.
- **Diverse** — one long-form piece, one short post, one internal doc, one email, one spoken-to-written transcript if available.
- **Stakes-real** — they wrote it because it mattered, not because they were polishing a portfolio. Unedited drafts are often more voice-accurate than published work.
- **First-person** — pieces written in their own voice, not quoting or interviewing.

## What to extract

### 1. Distinctive vocabulary (10 to 15 words or phrases)

Words they use that most people wouldn't. Not jargon — *their* jargon. Pet phrases. Characteristic hedges or refusals to hedge. Metaphors they return to.

*Example — for someone in HR:* "candidate" (never "applicant"). "teammate" (never "employee"). "sharp" for a good hire. "holding" for emotional labor. "underneath that" as a frequent transition.

### 2. Cadence patterns

- **Sentence length** — short and punchy? long with nested clauses? alternating? Most people have a signature rhythm.
- **Paragraph shape** — single lines? dense blocks? one-sentence paragraphs as emphasis?
- **Punctuation tells** — em-dashes? colons? semicolons? parentheticals? Some people never use exclamation points; some use them constantly.
- **Opening moves** — how do they start a piece? A question? A declarative? A name? Patterns repeat.

### 3. Posture

The stance underneath the words. Warm? Dry? Urgent? Playful? Precise? Tender? Skeptical? Frank?

A person can have a consistent posture across contexts *or* shift between 2 or 3. Note both. If they shift, note the trigger (writing to a peer vs. writing for an audience, e.g.).

### 4. Refusals

What this person *never* does in their writing. Often more identifying than what they do. Examples:

- Never uses listicles.
- Never says "utilize" when "use" works.
- Never apologizes for expertise.
- Never ends on a question.
- Never writes in second person.

### 5. Values signature

Phrases that carry their values, not just their style. The sentences that, stripped of context, you could still identify as them because of *what they mean*.

## What to preserve vs. what to polish

**Always preserve:**

- Their distinctive vocabulary, verbatim.
- Their sentence rhythm, even when AI-drafts would smooth it.
- Their refusals — do not introduce constructions they never use.
- Their metaphor set — stay inside their imagery, don't import fresh ones.
- Their posture, especially when the draft is about stakes-real work.

**Okay to polish:**

- Typos, obvious grammatical errors, broken parallels.
- Logical jumps that lose the reader — clarify without changing the line they were walking.
- Over-long sentences that have become genuinely unclear — break them at a natural breath, keep their vocabulary.
- Repetition that isn't rhetorical — trim, never reorder.

## The voice check (before any output ships)

Before Starlight produces a finished artifact, run this mental test:

> If this person read this aloud, would it sound like them — or like a generic consultant wrote it in their name?

If generic: rewrite. The whole substrate fails if Starlight's outputs sound like Starlight instead of the person.

## What the person should never have to do

The voice-extraction work is Starlight's job, not theirs. Do not ask the user to fill out a "voice worksheet" or describe their own style. People cannot accurately describe their own voice from the inside — that's exactly why Starlight is reflecting it back. Extract from corpus.

## When voice samples are thin

If the user has only provided 1 or 2 samples and wants a Profile or Pipeline, halt kindly:

> "I can see what you're showing me, but voice-cloning needs more than this. Could you drag in 3 more pieces? Something from a different context — an email you wrote that mattered, a doc you'd share internally, a post you drafted but didn't publish. Three more and I can give you a profile that actually sounds like you."

Never generate a generic-voice output to move the session along. That is the failure mode the substrate is built to prevent.

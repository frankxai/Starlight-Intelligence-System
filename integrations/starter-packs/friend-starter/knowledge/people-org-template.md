# People Org Template — Worked Example

> Reference-grade filled example for a 50-person consultancy (anonymized as "Meridian Strategy Partners") facing classic growing-pains: founder over-spanned at 12 directs, ambiguous middle layer, two key roles with no real succession plan. Includes role design for one ambiguous role, span audit for the whole org, trauma audit for a proposed reorg, and succession plan for two key roles.

---

## Disclaimer

This template is structural thinking architecture, not employment-law advice. All organizational changes implicate severance, classification, protected-class considerations, and jurisdictional variation. Engage qualified employment counsel before any action.

## The number you are operating against

**70%+ of reorgs fail to deliver promised outcomes; ~50% create more dysfunction than they solve.** This template is shaped by that constraint.

## Context — Meridian Strategy Partners

- **Industry:** boutique strategy consulting (financial-services and healthcare clients)
- **Headcount:** 50 (8 partners / principals + 12 senior consultants + 22 consultants/associates + 8 ops & support)
- **Stage:** scaling — 35% YoY headcount growth over last 18 months; revenue $18M, projected $24M
- **Location:** primary office NYC, secondary office London, ~30% remote
- **Founder:** Sarah K., founder/CEO, 12 years in role; ~80% of partner-level client relationships originate from her network
- **Current pain:**
  - Founder has 12 direct reports including 6 partners and 6 functional heads
  - Two key roles undefined: "Director of Practice Development" (the actual role of the person with that title is ambiguous; some say BD, some say internal capability-building, some say chief-of-staff to founder)
  - "Head of Healthcare Practice" hired 9 months ago; performance is mixed; role design was unclear at hire
  - No real succession plan for founder (5-year horizon question is live with the partner group)
  - No real succession plan for "Managing Partner, Financial Services" — incumbent is 64, signaled retirement intent within 24 months
- **Trauma history:** one prior re-org 26 months ago (consolidated 3 practice areas into 2). Outcome: mixed — operational efficiency improved, but two senior consultants left within 3 months citing "loss of identity in the new structure," and remaining staff still reference it as "the merge" with negative connotation.
- **Talent IS signals:** annual engagement survey 2 months ago — engagement at 72% (down from 78% the prior year); burnout signals concentrated in healthcare practice and in the senior-consultant layer (the squeezed middle).

## Section 1 — Role Design for the ambiguous "Director of Practice Development" role

### Current state

`ambiguous` — incumbent (David L., 4 years tenure, internally promoted) is a high-performer struggling to succeed because no one — including David — knows what the role actually decides or owns. Three different stakeholders describe the role three different ways:

- Founder: "He helps me with internal capability-building — running our knowledge management, training new consultants on our methodology"
- Managing Partners: "He's our BD lead — he should be opening new accounts and warming pipeline"
- David himself: "I'm trying to be useful in three directions and succeeding at none of them"

This is a textbook role-design failure causing performance to look like the problem when role design is the problem.

### Role Architecture — Director of Practice Development

#### Decision rights matrix

| Decision | David's authority | Escalation trigger |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Methodology / framework adoption decisions across the firm | Decides | When change requires partner billing-rate adjustment → Founder + Managing Partners |
| Consultant training curriculum (annual cycle) | Decides | When budget exceeds $200K → Founder |
| Knowledge management platform selection and operation | Decides | None below $100K annual cost |
| Hiring decisions for L&D / KM staff (his team) | Decides | None — his team |
| Pricing exceptions for client engagements | Consulted | Decision sits with engagement partner; David's input weighted on methodology-fit |
| New service line proposals | Recommends | Decision sits with founder + partner group; David's analysis required for any proposal |
| Speaking engagements / firm-representative external positioning | Recommends | Founder decides; David recommends based on positioning fit |
| Partner-level BD activity (account opening) | NOT IN ROLE | Removed from role (was implicit; explicitly removed) |

**Key resolution:** the role is *internal capability + methodology + knowledge*. It is NOT external BD. That ambiguity was the load-bearing failure. David is now structurally unable to succeed at BD because he is structurally not accountable for it.

#### Accountabilities (measurable outcomes)

1. **Consultant time-to-productivity** — new consultants reach billing target within 90 days, measured quarterly. Owner of: training curriculum, mentorship pairings, methodology onboarding. Dependent on: practice-leader engagement, recruiting quality (not in his control).

2. **Methodology adoption rate** — proprietary frameworks used in ≥80% of engagements where applicable, measured annually. Owner of: framework documentation, training, refresh cadence. Dependent on: partner buy-in, client receptivity.

3. **Knowledge re-use** — engagement teams reference prior work in ≥60% of new engagement plans, measured quarterly. Owner of: KM platform, search quality, capture discipline. Dependent on: consultant capture behavior.

4. **L&D NPS** — internal Net Promoter Score on training programs ≥ +40, measured semi-annually.

#### Success criteria

- **30 days:** the firm understands the role redefinition; David has met 1:1 with each partner to align on what is and is not in scope
- **60 days:** annual training curriculum published, methodology-refresh cadence proposed
- **90 days:** new-consultant onboarding redesign live for the next cohort
- **12 months:** time-to-productivity at target; methodology adoption tracked and reported quarterly; KM platform delivering measurable re-use

#### Escalation path

- Escalates to founder when budget exceeds $200K, when methodology change requires firm-wide pricing recalibration, or when a partner is actively undermining adoption
- Does NOT escalate (handles autonomously): KM platform operation, training curriculum delivery, mentorship program management, hiring of his own team
- Regular cadence: weekly 1:1 with founder (during stabilization period — first 6 months); biweekly thereafter

#### Interfaces with adjacent roles

| Adjacent role | Handoff in | Handoff out | Boundary |
|---------------|------------|-------------|----------|
| Practice Leaders | New methodology needs surfaced from engagement work | Methodology training, KM templates | Practice leaders own engagement delivery; David owns the methodology infrastructure |
| Recruiting | Training-readiness needs from incoming class | Onboarding curriculum spec | Recruiting owns hiring; David owns developing them post-hire |
| Founder | Strategic direction on capability investments | Methodology landscape report (quarterly) | Founder owns where firm is going; David owns how consultants get equipped to take it there |
| BD / Sales (where this exists) | Methodology positioning needs | Trained consultants ready to deliver | David is NOT BD; this boundary is now explicit |

#### Common failure modes

1. **Scope creep back into BD** — partners try to push BD activity onto David because the firm has not built a real BD function. Counter: founder publicly reaffirms the boundary at the next partner meeting; if BD capacity is the actual gap, run `/org-role-design` for a BD function or hire.
2. **Methodology becomes museum** — training and KM exist on paper but are not pressure-tested in live engagements. Counter: methodology-adoption metric, not just training-delivery metric.
3. **L&D NPS gaming** — training NPS goes up but consultant readiness does not. Counter: L&D NPS pairs with time-to-productivity (the harder metric).
4. **Founder regression** — when stressed, founder pulls David back into "help me think about X" chief-of-staff work. Counter: explicit no-go list; founder's chief-of-staff need is a different role and should be filled separately.

#### Anti-pattern flags (final check)

- [x] **RACI-only check:** decision rights are per-decision specific — PASS
- [x] **Measurable outcomes check:** every accountability has a measurable outcome — PASS
- [x] **Authority-responsibility match:** David has authority over inputs he's accountable for — PASS
- [x] **Title-scope match:** "Director of Practice Development" matches the redefined scope — PASS

### Composes with

- **Performance:** new criteria match new role design; David's last 12 months get re-read in light of the corrected role — what looked like underperformance was role-design failure
- **Hiring:** if firm needs BD capacity, that is now a separate ICP via `/hire-icp`
- **Talent IS:** monitor David's engagement during stabilization period (role redefinition is high-status, low-certainty for the role-holder)

## Section 2 — Span-of-Control Audit (whole org)

### Current span inventory

| Manager | Role | Directs | Work complexity | Span vs research bound |
|---------|------|---------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Sarah K. | Founder/CEO | 12 | Complex (mixed exec / partners / functional) | **Over-spanned** (research: 5-9 for complex; she's at 12) |
| Managing Partner FS | Practice head | 5 | Complex | Within bound |
| Managing Partner Healthcare | Practice head | 4 | Complex | Within bound (but role is itself struggling — separate issue) |
| Managing Partner Tech | Practice head | 3 | Complex | **Under-spanned** (signals scope-too-narrow or political accommodation) |
| COO | Operations | 6 | Mixed (HR + Finance + IT + Office) | Within bound |
| Director of Practice Dev (David) | L&D / KM | 2 | Mixed | **Under-spanned** (functional leader role; will grow to 4-5 with redesign) |
| Head of Recruiting | Recruiting | 2 | Mixed | **Under-spanned** |

### Pattern flags

- [x] **Top-heavy:** YES — founder at 12 directs doing complex work. Classic founder over-span pattern at this growth stage. Cost showing as: decision bottlenecks (partners report 2-3 week wait for founder availability on strategic questions), neglected reports (David is the prime example), founder burnout signals (Sarah has reported sleep issues, declining family time).
- [x] **Middle-thin:** PARTIAL — under-spanned managers below founder absorb work that should flow through layer-2. Tech practice managing partner with 3 directs is partly a political accommodation (he's a co-founder taking a step back; explicit, no judgment) but reduces effective leverage.
- [ ] Over-spanned ICs — none surfaced
- [x] **Under-spanned managers:** YES — David and Head of Recruiting; both are functional leaders whose teams need to grow if their function is to scale. This is fixable.
- [x] **Unbalanced layer:** YES — practice-head spans range from 3 to 5; defensible by practice size but worth tracking.
- [ ] Mixed-complexity over-span — not currently a flag

### Vision-fit check

Sarah's 3-year vision (per recent partner offsite): firm grows to 100 people, opens a third office (likely Singapore), launches a new healthcare-tech practice, and Sarah transitions to ~70% time on external positioning + key client relationships and ~30% on firm leadership. **The current span architecture cannot serve this vision.** Sarah at 12 directs cannot reduce to 30% firm-leadership time; the math doesn't work. A layer between her and the partners must exist.

### Redesign recommendations

#### Recommendation 1: Add a Chief of Staff role reporting to founder

- **Pattern this addresses:** top-heavy founder
- **Structural change:** new role (Chief of Staff) absorbs 4-5 of founder's directs (the functional heads — COO, David's role, Recruiting, plus one more). Partners continue to report directly to founder (no buffering of partners; they own the firm with her). Functional heads report through Chief of Staff who owns operational execution.
- **Headcount implication:** add 1 (the Chief of Staff hire)
- **Employment-law footprint:** none meaningful (additive role); flag for counsel only on compensation banding
- **Estimated wave:** Wave 2 (after role design)

#### Recommendation 2: Resolve Tech practice under-span structurally

- **Pattern this addresses:** under-spanned managing partner (political accommodation)
- **Structural change:** consolidate Tech practice into FS practice as a sub-vertical, OR formalize co-founder's reduced role with a different title that doesn't require running a practice. Decision is co-founder-led; Sarah's role is to make space for the conversation honestly.
- **Headcount implication:** none
- **Employment-law footprint:** title change for co-founder may have shareholder/voting implications; full counsel review required
- **Estimated wave:** Wave 3 (after Chief of Staff lands; co-founder conversation is delicate and shouldn't be bundled with the structural change)

#### Recommendation 3: Grow David's and Recruiting's teams to escape under-span

- **Pattern this addresses:** under-spanned functional leaders
- **Structural change:** David adds 2 hires (L&D Manager + KM Specialist); Recruiting adds 1-2 hires. Both team builds are budgeted; this is the operational cost of growth keeping pace.
- **Headcount implication:** add 3-4
- **Employment-law footprint:** standard hiring; no special flags
- **Estimated wave:** Wave 1 (these are immediate; underway in parallel with the structural moves)

### Sequencing — waves

- **Wave 1 (weeks 1-4):** role redesign for David's role (Section 1, above); approve Recruiting and L&D team-build hires
- **Wave 2 (weeks 4-12):** ICP and search for Chief of Staff; announce role to partner group at Wave 2 kickoff with full context
- **Wave 3 (weeks 12-20):** Chief of Staff onboards; founder's directs transition to Chief of Staff progressively over 60 days; co-founder conversation initiated separately
- **Wave 4 (months 5-12):** Talent IS pulse cadence (week 4 / 8 / 12 / 24 post-CoS-live); founder transitions to her 70/30 target time allocation

### Transition plan — per affected role

| Person/role | What changes | What stays | Told by | Told when | First-week structural support |
|-------------|-------------|------------|---------|-----------|------------------------------|
| David L. (Director Practice Dev) | Role redefined, decision rights specified, BD removed from scope | Title, comp, tenure | Sarah K. directly | Wave 1 kickoff | Weekly 1:1 with Sarah for first 6 months |
| COO | Reporting line moves from Sarah to Chief of Staff | Title, comp, scope | Sarah K. directly | Wave 2 announce, before public | Direct 1:1 with new CoS within 48 hours of CoS start |
| Head of Recruiting | Reporting line moves from Sarah to Chief of Staff; team grows by 1-2 | Title, comp, scope | Sarah K. directly | Wave 2 announce | Same as above |
| Tech practice MP (co-founder) | Conversation about role evolution; outcome TBD | Co-ownership, equity, voice | Sarah K. directly | Wave 3, not bundled with Wave 2 announce | Separate counsel review before conversation |

## Section 3 — Reorg Trauma Audit for the proposed structural reshape

### The number you are operating against

**70%+ of reorgs fail. This proposed reshape needs to land in the 30%.** The audit's job is to honestly tell you which side this sits on.

### Vision-fit pre-check

PASS. The 3-year vision (Section 2) cannot be served by the current span. This is genuinely a structural problem, not vision drift dressed as structure.

### Trauma history

| Date | Change | Scope | Outcome | Residual trust impact |
|------|--------|-------|---------|----------------------|
| 26 months ago | "The merge" — consolidated 3 practices to 2 | Department-level | Mixed (efficiency gained, 2 senior departures) | Moderate — staff still reference it negatively |

**Cumulative trauma score:** moderate — one reorg in last 3 years with mixed outcome. **Implication:** the next change carries higher cost than a baseline org would. Sequencing must be unusually careful.

### Current state read

- **Trust level (Talent IS):** 72% engagement (yellow — down 6 points from prior year)
- **Burnout signals:** concentrated in healthcare practice (Head of Healthcare role design issue) and in senior-consultant layer (squeezed middle)
- **Recent attrition:** 8% voluntary in last 12 months, no demographic concentration
- **Open performance issues at risk of being bundled:** Head of Healthcare. **DO NOT bundle this into the reorg.** Address via `/org-role-design` separately; bundling a performance issue into a structural change is a protected-class red flag waiting to happen.

### SCARF threat-activation forecast

| Dimension | Score (1-5) | Why this score |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Status | 2 | Most affected roles are growing in scope or being clarified; Tech MP conversation is the exception (handled in Wave 3, separately) |
| Certainty | 3 | Clear about end state; some uncertainty during Wave 2-3 about how reporting through CoS will feel in practice |
| Autonomy | 2 | Reporting-line change for COO and Recruiting is real but their decision authority within their domains is unchanged |
| Relatedness | 3 | New CoS is an outsider entering a tight partnership culture; integration risk is real |
| Fairness | 2 | Process is announced, sequenced, transparent; co-founder conversation is the area to watch |

**Aggregate threat score:** 12/25 — moderate. This is a manageable reorg if sequenced and communicated carefully.

### Conway's Law forecast

Adding a CoS layer between founder and operations will likely shift two things in the firm's operating cadence: (1) operational decisions will move faster (CoS owns them; founder doesn't bottleneck) but (2) strategic-operational integration will require explicit ritual (founder + CoS + COO weekly synthesis meeting) or operations will drift from strategic intent. Build the ritual into Wave 4.

### Refusal check

- [x] **Cosmetic?** PASS — substantive redesign with real decision-rights changes
- [x] **Surprise?** PASS — multi-month sequencing, full communication
- [x] **Flat-as-default?** PASS — adding a layer, not eliminating one; structural argument grounded in span research
- [x] **Vision drift dressed as structure?** PASS — vision-fit pre-check passed

Reorg proceeds.

### Communication architecture

**Kotter scaffolding:**
- **Urgency:** Sarah at 12 directs cannot deliver the 3-year vision; the math is real. Honest framing including the 70% reorg-failure rate.
- **Coalition:** Sarah + 2 most senior partners + COO. Visible, unanimous, public at Wave 2 kickoff.
- **Vision:** 3-year picture: 100 people, third office, healthcare-tech practice, Sarah at 70/30. CoS is the structural enabler.
- **Communicate:** all-hands at Wave 2 announce; weekly written update during Waves 2-3; monthly thereafter.
- **Empower:** removing meeting-load from founder explicitly; CoS empowered to decide on operational items below named threshold.
- **Wins:** first visible win = founder calendar opens up by ~10 hours/week within 60 days of CoS landing; named and celebrated.
- **Consolidate:** no early-victory declaration before month 6.
- **Institutionalize:** updated org chart in firm wiki; updated decision-rights matrix; updated weekly cadence.

**Bridges transitions overlay:**
- **Ending phase (Wave 1-2):** acknowledge what's ending — direct access to founder for functional heads; informal "Sarah-decides-everything-eventually" rhythm. Make space for the loss of that intimacy.
- **Neutral zone (Wave 3, weeks 12-20):** the productive-uncomfortable middle. CoS is new. Reporting feels weird. Decisions feel like they're going through a buffer. Normalize this. Weekly comms.
- **New beginning (month 6+):** declared after the cadence has settled, the wins are visible, and the team can articulate the new rhythm.

### Per-team mitigation

| Team | Concentrated SCARF threats | Message delivered by | Support provided | First-30-day structural support |
|------|---------------------------|---------------------|------------------|------------------------------|
| Functional heads (COO, David, Recruiting) | Status (perceived demotion to "report through CoS") | Sarah directly, individually, before any public comm | Reaffirm scope and authority within domains | Direct 1:1 with new CoS within 48 hours; founder remains skip-level monthly |
| Partners | Relatedness (cohesion concern with new layer) | Sarah at next partner offsite | Reaffirm partners report direct to Sarah, not through CoS | Standing partner meeting cadence preserved |
| Tech practice MP (co-founder) | Status (separate Wave 3 conversation) | Sarah privately, with counsel pre-briefed | Open conversation about co-founder's preferred next chapter | Separate process, not bundled |

### Aftercare monitoring

- **Talent IS pulse:** week 4 / 8 / 12 / 24 / 36 post-CoS-live
- **Pause-and-diagnose trigger:** if functional-head engagement drops >15% OR healthcare practice burnout signals worsen → halt Wave 4 expansion, diagnose
- **12-month honest review:** did founder hit 70/30 time allocation? Did operational cadence speed up? Did strategic-operational integration ritual hold? Honest answer.

## Section 4 — Succession Plan for two key roles

### The readiness standard

Real succession requires named successor + readiness gap + 6-12 month dev plan + tested partial in-role + named timeline + transparent communication. Anything missing = paper.

### Critical role inventory

| Role | Why critical | 30-day risk | 90-day risk | 12-month risk |
|------|--------------|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| Founder/CEO (Sarah) | 80% of partner-relationships originate from her network; firm identity is partly hers | Major client unease; partner anxiety | Slowdown in new client acquisition; partner confidence test | Strategic drift if no acting transition plan |
| Managing Partner Financial Services (Mark, 64) | Largest practice ($9M revenue); 30 years of FS-industry relationships; signaled retirement within 24 months | Practice continuity question; FS-client confidence | Practice-strategy drift; partner-team realignment | If unmanaged, ~30% revenue exposure |

### Role 1: Founder/CEO

- **Incumbent:** Sarah K., founder/CEO
- **Named successor (primary):** TO BE NAMED — currently no named internal successor. This is the load-bearing finding of the audit.
- **Named successor (secondary):** N/A pending primary

**Honest assessment:** Sarah's succession is a 5-year question, not a 1-year question. But because no internal candidate is currently in development, the 5-year question becomes a 7-9 year question, and that compresses against Sarah's own life plans (which include reduced firm time starting in year 3-4).

#### Development path (immediate)

The primary task right now is not to *name* a successor (premature). It is to *develop* 2-3 candidates over 24-36 months so a named successor can be selected from a real bench at month 36, with full transition by month 60. Candidates likely come from the Managing Partner pool plus possibly the new CoS hire (if CoS has CEO-track potential, which is part of the ICP).

#### Tested partial in-role (immediate steps)

Sarah begins delegating specific founder-level activities to candidate partners in controlled rotations:
- Lead the next quarterly partner offsite (one rotation each)
- Run the firm's annual planning cycle (one rotation)
- Serve as primary spokesperson for one major external event per quarter (rotations)

Each partner-rotation tracks against a named readiness rubric (decision quality, partner-cohesion impact, external positioning, strategic clarity).

#### Named timeline

- Month 0-12: development rotations begin; rubric in place
- Month 12: first readiness review of each candidate
- Month 18-24: narrow to 2 candidates; deeper rotations
- Month 36: named successor announced internally to partner group
- Month 48-60: progressive transition; Sarah moves to Chair role

#### Transparent communication

- Sarah names the development plan to the partner group at the next offsite (board / shareholder communication ahead of partner comm if applicable; counsel review)
- Each partner knows they are in the development pool (silent succession is the trust-erosion failure mode here)

### Role 2: Managing Partner Financial Services (Mark)

- **Incumbent:** Mark, 64, signaled retirement intent within 24 months
- **Named successor (primary):** Jennifer T., Senior Partner FS, 8 years tenure, leads the firm's largest single account
- **Named successor (secondary):** Robert C., Senior Partner FS, 5 years tenure (development path; not ready in 24 months but in 4-5 years)

#### Readiness gap assessment — Jennifer

| Network | Has half of Mark's external relationships; the other half is institutional | Months 1-18 — formal introduction tour with Mark |
| Decision-making | Has not made hire/fire decisions at partner level | Month 6 — Jennifer co-leads the next partner-level hiring decision |

#### 6-12-month development plan

| Element | Specifics | Cadence |
|---------|-----------|---------|

| Stretch assignments | Co-lead practice-strategy planning Q2-Q3; sole lead on next partner-pipeline review | as scheduled |
| Relationships | Joint-meeting protocol with Mark for top 12 FS clients over 12 months | weekly to monthly |
| Sponsor | Mark + Sarah jointly | monthly check-in |
| Quarterly readiness review | Mark + Sarah + Jennifer | quarterly |

#### Tested partial in-role

| Test | What Jennifer does | When | Pass criteria |
|------|------------------|------|---------------|
| Run the practice for 4 weeks while Mark is on planned sabbatical | Full practice leadership including partner team and largest two clients | Month 8 | No client confidence loss measured by post-period client check-ins; partner team rates leadership ≥7/10 |
| Lead next practice quarterly review without Mark in the room | Strategy + financial + people review | Month 10 | Sarah rates ≥ "ready to assume role with continued advisor support" |
| Independently negotiate one major client renewal | Renewal of top-5 FS client | Month 12 | Successful renewal at retention or expansion terms |

#### Named timeline

- Months 0-12: development plan and tested partials
- Month 12: readiness review; if pass → transition begins
- Months 12-18: progressive transition; Jennifer assumes role with Mark as advisor
- Month 18-24: full transition; Mark to advisor / part-time role per his preference
- Adjustment criteria: if month-12 review shows gaps unclosed, extend development by 6 months; if material gap, surface to Sarah for re-evaluation

#### Transparent communication

- [x] Jennifer has been told (Mark and Sarah, joint conversation, last month)
- [x] Mark knows the timeline and is aligned (initiated by Mark)
- [x] Robert (secondary) has been told he is in the development pool but not the named primary; this is non-negotiable transparency
- [ ] Partner-group disclosure: at next partner offsite (in 6 weeks), with Mark + Sarah delivering jointly
- [ ] Top-12 FS clients: relationship-introduction begins immediately; explicit succession framing at Month 6

### Aggregate succession bench-strength

- **Bench depth:** 1 of 2 critical roles has named, ready-track successor (Mark/Jennifer); 1 of 2 needs development cohort started (Sarah)
- **Bench breadth:** 4 senior partners across practices are succession-development-eligible; 2 are committed to development paths; 2 are uncertain about appetite for senior-leadership trajectory (separate conversations needed)
- **Concentration risk:** moderate — Jennifer is in two succession plans (FS practice + future CEO development pool). Acceptable but watched.

### Risk flags

- [x] **Single-point-of-failure:** Founder/CEO role has no named successor; URGENT to start development cohort
- [ ] **Unprepared successors:** Jennifer's gaps are real but development plan is real; PASS
- [ ] **Undisclosed succession:** Jennifer + Robert + partner group all transparent; PASS
- [x] **Founder-shaped role:** Founder/CEO role is shaped around Sarah's specific capabilities; before naming a successor, run `/org-role-design` for the post-Sarah CEO role to clarify what is structural vs what is Sarah-specific
- [x] **Protected-class promotion-pattern check:** review pattern of who is in development pools across critical roles; current pool skews male and white; actively widen the pool with intention; flag for legal counsel review on patterns
